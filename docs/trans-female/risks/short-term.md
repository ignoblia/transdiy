# Short-Term Side Effects

This page covers the common short-term side effects that may occur in the first weeks and months of feminising HRT. Most are temporary and manageable.

## Common Side Effects

### Breast Tenderness

| Details | Management |
|---------|------------|
| One of the first signs estrogen is working | Wear a soft, supportive bra or bralette |
| Usually begins within 2–4 weeks | Avoid sleeping on your stomach |
| Can be sharp or aching | Usually subsides after a few months |
| Normalises as breast development progresses | If pain is severe or one-sided, see a doctor |

### Nausea

| Details | Management |
|---------|------------|
| Common with oral estrogen in first few weeks | Take with food |
| Usually resolves within 2–4 weeks | Try sublingual administration |
| Less common with transdermal/injectable | Ginger tea or anti-nausea meds if needed |

### Fatigue / Low Energy

| Details | Management |
|---------|------------|
| Common in the first few weeks | Rest when needed |
| Body adjusting to new hormone balance | Ensure adequate sleep (7–9 hours) |
| Can be worse with CPA | Usually resolves within 1–2 months |
| | Rule out anaemia (check CBC on blood test) |

### Dizziness (Spironolactone)

| Details | Management |
|---------|------------|
| Due to blood pressure lowering effect | Take spiro with food |
| Most common in first 2 weeks | Stay hydrated — increase salt and water intake |
| Usually resolves as body adjusts | Stand up slowly from sitting/lying down |
| | If persistent, ask provider about dose reduction |

### Frequent Urination (Spironolactone)

| Details | Management |
|---------|------------|
| Expected — spiro is a diuretic | Take dose earlier in the day |
| Usually improves somewhat over time | Avoid taking close to bedtime |
| More noticeable at higher doses | Stay hydrated |

### Hot Flashes

| Details | Management |
|---------|------------|
| Can occur when T is dropping quickly | Usually temporary (1–4 weeks) |
| Also seen with GnRH agonists | Ensure E2 is in adequate range |
| | If persistent, check hormone levels |

### Mood Changes

| Details | Management |
|---------|------------|
| Emotional lability is common early on | Give yourself grace — your brain is adapting |
| Can include crying more easily | Talk to supportive friends/community |
| Irritability or anxiety possible | If severe or persistent, talk to a therapist |
| | If CPA is the cause, consider switching |

## Less Common Short-Term Side Effects

| Side Effect | Likely Cause | What to Do |
|-------------|--------------|------------|
| Headaches | Estrogen dose changes; blood pressure | Monitor; check BP; hydrate |
| Acne (temporary) | Hormonal fluctuation | Usually resolves; gentle skincare |
| Fluid retention / bloating | Estrogen effect | Reduce sodium; stay hydrated |
| Changes in body temperature regulation | Metabolic shift | Layer clothing; adjust thermostat |
| Changes in sense of smell | Hormonal | Generally harmless |

## When to Seek Medical Attention

::: danger Emergency warning signs
Seek immediate medical attention if you experience:

- **Sudden shortness of breath** — could be pulmonary embolism (PE)
- **Chest pain** — could be PE or heart-related
- **Leg pain/swelling/redness** (especially one leg) — could be deep vein thrombosis (DVT)
- **Sudden severe headache** — could be stroke or meningioma-related
- **Vision changes** — double vision, blurring — could be meningioma (CPA-related)
- **Severe depression or suicidal thoughts** — seek mental health crisis support
- **Signs of allergic reaction** — rash, swelling, difficulty breathing (rare)
:::

## Side Effects Checklist (First 3 Months)

| Week | What to Expect |
|------|----------------|
| Week 1–2 | Nausea (pills), dizziness (spiro), frequent urination |
| Week 2–4 | Breast tenderness begins; mood changes; skin softening |
| Week 4–8 | Fatigue improving; libido decreasing; reduced erections |
| Month 2–3 | Most short-term side effects stabilise or resolve |
