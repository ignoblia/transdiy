# Verifying Product Quality

## Why Verification Matters

Counterfeit, under-dosed, or contaminated HRT medications are a real risk — particularly when buying from unregulated sources. Using poor-quality medication can lead to:

- No hormone level changes (wasted money, no results)
- Unsafe ingredients (contamination, allergic reactions)
- Unexpected side effects
- Delayed transition progress

## Visual Inspection

### Pills

| Check | What Genuine Looks Like |
|-------|------------------------|
| **Shape and size** | Consistent; no chips or cracks |
| **Colour** | Uniform; matches known reference |
| **Markings** | Clear imprint codes (e.g., "E 2") |
| **Packaging** | Blister packs with batch number and expiry date |
| **Expiry date** | Present and legible |

### Injectables (Vials)

| Check | What to Look For |
|-------|------------------|
| **Solution clarity** | Clear, colourless or slightly yellow — no cloudiness |
| **No particles** | No floating specks, fibres, or crystals |
| **Seal** | Crimp cap is intact; no signs of tampering |
| **Labelling** | Clear label with concentration, ester, batch number, date |
| **Storage** | Stored at room temperature, away from light |

## At-Home Testing

### Water Test (Pills)

Some estradiol pills will sink or behave differently in water than counterfeits — but this is **not reliable** and should not be your primary verification method.

### Blood Test Verification

The **most reliable** way to verify your medication is to check your blood levels:

- Start at a known dose
- Test E2 (trough) after 4–6 weeks
- If E2 levels are far below what the dose should produce, the medication may be under-dosed or counterfeit

For example: 4 mg/day of genuine oral estradiol should produce E2 levels of roughly 60–100 pg/mL (trough). If you get < 30 pg/mL, something is wrong.

## Third-Party Testing

Some community organisations or suppliers offer:

- **HPLC testing** — quantitative analysis of estradiol content
- **Sterility testing** — checking for bacterial/endotoxin contamination

These are the gold standard for verification but require sending samples to a lab.

## Batch Numbers and Authentication

- Legitimate pharmaceutical products have batch/lot numbers
- Some manufacturers have online verification portals
- If the batch number is illegible or missing, this is a red flag

## Red Flags Summary

| ❌ Red Flag | Why |
|------------|-----|
| Price far below market average | Likely counterfeit or under-dosed |
| No photos of actual product | Seller may not have the product |
| Refuses to share batch info | Hiding something |
| Packaging looks generic/plain | Not from a legitimate manufacturer |
| Multiple bad reviews | Trust the community consensus |
| Seller pressures you to buy quickly | Scam tactic |
