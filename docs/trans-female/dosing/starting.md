# Starting Doses (Beginner)

## Before You Start

::: danger Get a baseline blood test first
Before taking any hormones, get a baseline blood test including:
- E2 (estradiol)
- Total T (testosterone)
- LH, FSH
- SHBG
- CBC (complete blood count)
- Comprehensive metabolic panel (liver, kidney function)
- Lipid panel

This gives you a reference point to measure changes against.
:::

::: info Doses below are for **adults (18+)**.
Adolescent-specific guidance is provided in the section above.
:::

## Adolescent Dosing (Under 18)

Adolescent hormone therapy follows a **start low, go slow** approach to mimic the gradual pace of endogenous puberty. These guidelines are based on WPATH SOC8 and Endocrine Society (2017) recommendations.

::: warning Important
This guide does not recommend DIY HRT for minors without medical supervision. Hormone therapy in adolescents ideally involves a multidisciplinary team including a qualified provider. The information below is provided for educational purposes and to help adolescents and families understand standard protocols.<a id="ref-adol-1"></a>
:::

### General Principles for Adolescents

- **Pre-pubertal children**: Hormone therapy is **not recommended** — social transition and supportive care are the standard of care <a id="ref-adol-2"></a>.
- **Tanner stage 2+**: Puberty blockers (GnRH agonists) are the first-line intervention if suppression of natal puberty is desired <a id="ref-adol-3"></a>.
- **Gender-affirming hormones** are typically added around age 14–16, after a multidisciplinary team confirms persistence of gender incongruence and the adolescent has sufficient capacity for informed consent <a id="ref-adol-4"></a>.
- The goal is to **mimic pubertal hormone levels**, gradually increasing over 2–3 years until adult doses are reached <a id="ref-adol-5"></a>.

### Estrogen — Pubertal Induction (Early to Mid Puberty)

For adolescents in early puberty (Tanner stage 2–3), estrogen is introduced at very low doses and increased every 6 months:

| Phase | Oral Estradiol | Transdermal Estradiol | Duration |
|-------|---------------|----------------------|----------|
| Starting dose | 0.25–0.5 mg/day (or 5 µg/kg/day) | 6.25–12.5 µg/24h (¼–½ of a 25 µg patch) | 6 months |
| Step 2 | 0.5–1 mg/day (or 10 µg/kg/day) | 25 µg/24h | 6 months |
| Step 3 | 1–2 mg/day (or 15 µg/kg/day) | 37.5 µg/24h | 6 months |
| Step 4 | 2–4 mg/day (or 20 µg/kg/day) | 50 µg/24h | 6 months |
| Adult maintenance | 2–6 mg/day | 50–200 µg/24h | Ongoing |

*Doses per Endocrine Society (2017) pubertal induction protocol <a id="ref-adol-8"></a> and Hopkins Adolescent Dosing Guide <a id="ref-adol-9"></a>.*

### Estrogen — Post-Pubertal Adolescents (Tanner Stage 5)

Adolescents who have completed natal puberty can increase doses more rapidly:

| Timeline | Oral Estradiol | Transdermal Estradiol |
|----------|---------------|----------------------|
| Months 0–6 | 1 mg/day | 25–50 µg/24h |
| Month 6+ | 2 mg/day | 50–100 µg/24h |
| Adult maintenance | 2–6 mg/day | 50–200 µg/24h |

### Anti-Androgens in Adolescents

| Medication | Adolescent Dosing | Notes |
|------------|------------------|-------|
| **Spironolactone** | Start 25 mg/day; titrate up slowly to max 100–200 mg/day | Monitor potassium and blood pressure. Lower starting doses than adults. |
| **GnRH agonist** | Per protocol (e.g., leuprolide 11.25 mg IM q3m or 22.5 mg SC q3m) | First-line for adolescents. Fully reversible. Prescription only. <a id="ref-adol-6"></a> |
| **CPA** | Generally **not recommended** in adolescents due to meningioma risk with long-term use | Risk-benefit should be carefully evaluated <a id="ref-adol-7"></a> |

### Age-Based Summary

| Age Range | Typical Approach |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Under 13** | Social transition + supportive care. Puberty blockers at Tanner stage 2, if indicated. |
| **13–15** | Puberty blockers if not already started. Low-dose estrogen (pubertal induction protocol). Increase slowly every 6 months. |
| **16–17** | May follow post-pubertal protocol (more rapid increase). Full adult doses may be appropriate by age 17–18, guided by levels and response. |
| **18+** | Adult dosing as below. |

## Starting Estrogen Regimens (Adults)

Choose **one** estrogen route:

### Option A: Oral/Sublingual Pills

| Medication | Starting Dose | Notes |
|------------|---------------|-------|
| Estradiol hemihydrate (e.g., Estrofem) | 2 mg/day (1 mg twice daily) | Split doses for stable levels |
| Estradiol valerate (e.g., Progynova) | 2 mg/day | Split twice daily |

### Option B: Transdermal Patches

| Strength | Starting Dose | Notes |
|----------|---------------|-------|
| Patches (e.g., Estradot, Climara) | 50–100 mcg/24h | Change twice weekly or weekly per brand |

### Option C: Gel

| Brand | Starting Dose | Notes |
|-------|---------------|-------|
| Oestrogel | 2–3 pumps/day | Apply to upper arms/shoulders |
| Sandrena | 1 mg/day | Apply to thighs/abdomen |

### Option D: Injections

| Medication | Starting Dose | Notes |
|------------|---------------|-------|
| EV (estradiol valerate) | 2–4 mg every 5 days | Short half-life |
| EC (estradiol cypionate) | 1–3 mg every 7 days | Lower starting dose than EV due to longer half-life |
| EEn (estradiol enanthate) | 4 mg every 7 days | Longer half-life |

## Starting Anti-Androgen Regimens (Adults)

Choose **one** anti-androgen (or none if attempting monotherapy):

| Medication | Starting Dose | Notes |
|------------|---------------|-------|
| **Spironolactone** | 25–50 mg/day | Increase to 100 mg/day after 2–4 weeks |
| **CPA** | 6.25–12.5 mg/day | Start at lowest effective dose; **do not exceed 12.5 mg/day** |
| **Bicalutamide** | 25–50 mg/day | No titration needed |
| **GnRH agonist** | Per prescribing protocol | Prescription only |

## Sample Starting Regimens

### Standard Regimen (Pills)

```
Estradiol hemihydrate 1 mg twice daily (2 mg/day total)
Spironolactone 50 mg once daily
```

### Standard Regimen (Patches) — Lowest VTE Risk

```
Estradiol patch 50 mcg/24h, change twice weekly
Bicalutamide 25–50 mg once daily
```

### Low-Risk Regimen (Monotherapy Attempt)

```
Estradiol valerate 4 mg every 5 days (IM or SC)
No anti-androgen
```

### CPA-Based Regimen (Common in EU/UK)

```
Estradiol gel 2–3 pumps/day
CPA 10–12.5 mg/day
```

## What to Expect in the First Month

| Week | Expected Changes |
|------|------------------|
| Week 1 | Little to no physical change; possible nausea or dizziness |
| Week 2 | Breast tenderness may begin; libido starts to drop |
| Week 3 | Skin feels softer; emotional changes possible |
| Week 4 | Reduced erections; breast buds may be palpable |

## When to Increase Dose

- **Estrogen:** After 4–8 weeks, if E2 is below target (100–200 pg/mL) and you have no side effects
- **Anti-androgen:** After 4–8 weeks, if T is above 50 ng/dL and the AA is well tolerated
- **Increase slowly** — do not double doses without blood test confirmation

## Common Starting Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's a Problem |
|---------|-------------------|
| Starting too high | More side effects; harder to know what works |
| Starting too many meds at once | Can't tell which is causing side effects |
| No baseline blood test | Can't measure progress |
| Taking oral estrogen once daily | Levels peak then crash; split doses |
| Expecting changes too fast | HRT is a marathon, not a sprint |
