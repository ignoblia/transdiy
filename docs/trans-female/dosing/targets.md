# Target Ranges (E2, T, E1, SHBG)

## Primary Targets

The following targets are based on Endocrine Society guidelines and WPATH SOC8 recommendations:

| Hormone | Target Range | Why |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| **Estradiol (E2)** | **100–200 pg/mL (367–734 pmol/L)** | Feminisation, bone health, T suppression |
| **Testosterone (T)** | **< 50 ng/dL (< 1.73 nmol/L)** | Suppress masculine traits |
| **SHBG** | **40–120 nmol/L** (normal); > 150 nmol/L indicates excess binding | High SHBG binds free hormones, reducing effectiveness |

## Secondary Targets

| Hormone | Target Range | Notes |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| **Estrone (E1)** | Not routinely needed | WPATH SOC8 states E1:E2 ratios need not be measured. Recent evidence does not support the claim that high E1:E2 ratios impair feminisation. Switching to parenteral routes avoids high E1. |
| **Free T** | Below female range upper limit | Roughly < 3 ng/dL |
| **DHT** | < 10 ng/dL | May be relevant if hair loss continues despite low T |
| **LH** | < 2 IU/L | Confirms HPG axis suppression |
| **FSH** | < 2 IU/L | Confirms HPG axis suppression |

## Understanding the Numbers

### Estradiol (E2)

| Level | Interpretation | Action |
|-------|---------------|--------|
| < 50 pg/mL | Ineffective | Increase dose |
| 50–100 pg/mL | Suboptimal | Increase dose |
| **100–200 pg/mL** | **Optimal target** | Maintain |
| 200–300 pg/mL | Elevated but acceptable (monotherapy) | OK if T is suppressed and SHBG normal |
| > 300 pg/mL (trough) | Excessive | Reduce dose (unless monotherapy and well-tolerated) |

### Testosterone (T)

| Level | Interpretation | Action |
|-------|---------------|--------|
| > 200 ng/dL | Not suppressed | Increase AA or E2 |
| 100–200 ng/dL | Partially suppressed | Increase AA |
| 50–100 ng/dL | Borderline | Consider increasing AA |
| **< 50 ng/dL** | **Target achieved** | Maintain |
| < 20 ng/dL | Fully suppressed (fine) | Could try reducing AA if side effects |

## How Route Affects Levels

| Route | Typical E2 at Standard Dose | E1:E2 Ratio | SHBG Impact |
|-------|---------------------------|-------------|-------------|
| Oral pills | 30–60 pg/mL per 2 mg | ~5:1 (high E1) | Raises SHBG |
| Sublingual | 80–200 pg/mL per 2 mg | ~1:1 | Moderate SHBG rise |
| Transdermal patches | Steady 50–150 pg/mL | ~0.5:1 (lower E1) | Minimal SHBG rise |
| Gel | Steady 50–150 pg/mL | ~0.5:1 | Minimal SHBG rise |
| Injectable EV | Peak high, trough variable | ~0.5:1 | Minimal SHBG rise |

## Target Timing

### When to Test

Always test at **trough** — the lowest point in your dosing cycle:

| Route | Trough Is |
|-------|-----------|
| Oral/sublingual (daily) | Just before next dose (e.g., morning before taking pills) |
| Transdermal patches | Day of change (just before applying new patch) |
| Gel | Before morning application |
| Injectable EV | Day 5 (just before next injection) |
| Injectable EC | Day 7 |
| Injectable EEn | Day 7 |

## The Problem with High SHBG

SHBG binds to sex hormones and makes them inactive. High SHBG can mean:

- **High total E2** but **low free E2** (less feminisation)
- **High total T** (from bicalutamide) but **low free T** (good, actually)
- **Insensitivity to dose increases** — more E2 just raises SHBG further

### Causes of High SHBG

- Oral estrogen (especially at high doses)
- Genetics
- Thyroid conditions
- Liver conditions
- Low androgen levels

### What to Do About High SHBG

| SHBG Level | Recommendation |
|------------|---------------|
| < 80 nmol/L | Normal; no action needed |
| 80–150 nmol/L | Mild elevation; monitor |
| > 150 nmol/L | Consider switching from oral to transdermal/injectable E2 |

## When Targets Don't Tell the Whole Story

Some trans women have levels in target range but see poor feminisation. Possible reasons:

- **High SHBG** — free hormones too low (see above)
- **Slow response genetics** — some people are just slow responders
- **Androgen receptor sensitivity** — rare; some people are hypersensitive to residual androgens
- **Progesterone needed** — may help breast maturation
- **Too early** — HRT takes years, not months

Conversely, some trans women see good changes at E2 levels below 100 pg/mL. Targets are guidelines, not absolute rules.
