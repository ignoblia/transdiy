# Fertility & Preservation

## How HRT Affects Fertility

Feminising HRT (estrogen + anti-androgens) suppresses testosterone production, which reduces sperm production. The effect is:

| Duration on HRT | Fertility Impact |
|-----------------|------------------|
| 0–3 months | Gradually declining |
| 3–12 months | Significantly reduced |
| 1+ years | Usually very low to zero |
| Long-term | May be permanent |

::: warning Individual variation is significant
Some trans women remain fertile even after years on HRT. Others become infertile within months. There is no way to predict individual outcomes.
:::

## Is HRT a Contraceptive?

**No.** HRT is not reliable contraception. Pregnancy is possible even while on HRT, particularly in the first year or if your hormone levels are not fully suppressed.

::: danger Use contraception if pregnancy is a concern
If you have a partner who can get you pregnant, use a reliable method of birth control (condoms, partner's contraception, etc.) even if you are on HRT.
:::

## Fertility Preservation Options

### Before Starting HRT (Ideal)

| Option | What It Involves | Cost |
|--------|------------------|------|
| **Sperm banking** | Collect and freeze sperm samples | $300–1,000 initial; $100–300/year storage |
| **Testicular tissue freezing** | Experimental; not widely available | Varies |

### While on HRT (Possible but Less Reliable)

- Sperm quality declines on HRT, so banking after starting may yield lower-quality samples
- Some trans women pause HRT for 3–6 months to produce viable sperm
- "Cryopreservation on HRT" is possible but less successful

### Pausing HRT for Fertility

If you want to pause HRT to preserve fertility:

1. Stop estrogen and anti-androgens
2. Testosterone will return to pre-HRT levels over 2–6 months
3. Sperm production typically resumes within 3–6 months
4. Once sperm is banked, you can restart HRT

::: warning Pausing HRT can be emotionally difficult
T temporarily returns, which can cause dysphoria and masculinisation changes. Weight the emotional cost carefully. Some trans women find it manageable; others do not.
:::

## Options for Having Children

| Option | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Sperm bank → IVF/IUI** | Use banked sperm with partner or surrogate |
| **Adoption** | Becoming a parent through adoption |
| **Foster care** | Temporary or permanent fostering |
| **No children** | A valid and fulfilling choice |

## Note on Pregnancy

Trans women cannot become pregnant. If you want to carry a pregnancy, that is not currently possible with existing medical technology (uterus transplant is experimental and not available for trans women).
