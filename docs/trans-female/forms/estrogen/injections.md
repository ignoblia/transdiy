# Injectable Estrogen

## Overview

Injectable estradiol is a popular choice for DIY HRT and is increasingly prescribed. It provides high bioavailability, excellent T suppression (often enabling monotherapy), and requires less frequent dosing than pills or gel.

## Estradiol Esters

| Ester | Half-Life | Typical Dose | Frequency |
|-------|-----------|--------------|-----------|
| **Estradiol valerate (EV)** | 4–5 days | 2–8 mg | Every 5 days |
| **Estradiol cypionate (EC)** | 5–7 days | 1–5 mg | Every 7 days |
| **Estradiol enanthate (EEn)** | 7–10 days | 3–8 mg | Every 7 days |
| **Estradiol undecylate (EUn)** | 12–16 days | 10–20 mg | Every 14–28 days |

::: tip EEn availability
Estradiol enanthate (EEn) is significantly less available as a pharmaceutical product than EV or EC in the UK, most of Europe, and North America. It is more commonly sourced via homebrew. If you need a pharmaceutical option, EV or EC are more widely stocked.
:::

## Monotherapy with Injections

One of the biggest advantages of injectable estradiol is that at sufficient doses, it can suppress testosterone **without anti-androgens** (monotherapy). This works because:

- High E2 levels signal the hypothalamus/pituitary to reduce LH production
- Lower LH means the testes produce less testosterone
- At E2 trough levels above ~200 pg/mL, most people achieve T < 50 ng/dL

::: tip Monotherapy dosing
For EV, most people achieve T suppression at 4–6 mg every 5 days. For EEn, 4–6 mg every 7 days. EC typically requires slightly lower dosing (3–5 mg every 7 days). Recent large studies (2024-2025) show the median effective dose is around 4 mg weekly, and doses above 6 mg weekly often produce supraphysiologic E2 levels without additional T suppression benefit. Check trough levels after 4–6 weeks and adjust accordingly.
:::

## Injection Technique

### Equipment Needed

- Estradiol vial
- Syringe (1–3 mL, luer lock recommended)
- Needles: 18–23G for drawing, 25–27G for injecting
- Alcohol swabs
- Sharps container

### Injection Sites

| Site | Needle Length | Notes |
|------|--------------|-------|
| **Gluteal (buttock)** | 1–1.5 inches | IM; most common, steady absorption |
| **Ventrogluteal** | 1–1.5 inches | IM; lower pain, good absorption |
| **Thigh (vastus lateralis)** | 1–1.5 inches | IM; easy to self-inject |
| **Subcutaneous (abdomen)** | 0.5–0.625 inches | SC; slower absorption but effective |

### Step-by-Step (IM)

1. Wash hands thoroughly
2. Clean the vial rubber stopper with an alcohol swab
3. Draw air into syringe (equal to your dose volume)
4. Inject air into the vial
5. Draw the correct dose volume
6. Change to injection needle
7. Clean injection site with alcohol swab
8. Insert needle at 90° angle (IM)
9. Do not aspirate (pull back plunger) — modern guidelines from the CDC, WHO, and AMA recommend against aspiration for IM and SC injections as it does not improve safety and adds unnecessary pain. If you see blood in the syringe after insertion, you have hit a vessel — withdraw, apply pressure, and try a new site with a fresh needle.
10. Inject slowly
11. Withdraw needle, apply pressure
12. Dispose of needle safely in sharps container

## Injection Frequency & Trough Levels

- **Test at trough** — right before your next injection (lowest point of your cycle)
- **Target trough E2**: 100–200 pg/mL per guidelines (higher for monotherapy, typically 200–300 pg/mL)
- **Target T**: < 50 ng/dL
- Test after at least 4 weeks on a stable dose
- Note: Some people achieve T suppression even with E2 < 100 pg/mL, and not everyone with E2 above 200 pg/mL achieves T < 50 ng/dL. Individual response varies significantly.

## Pros & Cons

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| High bioavailability | Requires needles (fear of injections) |
| Excellent T suppression (monotherapy) | More complicated to do |
| Low VTE risk (no first-pass) | Requires sterile technique |
| Less frequent dosing | Injection site pain/swelling |
| Best E2:E1 ratio | Estradiol vials less commonly prescribed |
| Good for stubborn T | Can cause high peaks + low troughs |

## Common Problems

| Problem | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| Pain at injection site | Use smaller needle (27G), warm the vial, inject slowly |
| Lumps (oil deposits) | Massage area, rotate injection sites |
| Post-injection bleeding | Apply pressure; use smaller needle |
| Air bubbles | Tap syringe and push out bubbles before injecting |
| Can't draw the solution | Inject air into vial first |
