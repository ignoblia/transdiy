# Oral Estrogen (Pills)

## Overview

Oral estradiol is the most commonly prescribed form of estrogen for feminising HRT. It is taken as a pill (swallowed), metabolised by the liver, and provides a steady baseline level of estradiol.

## Available Forms

| Medication | Common Brand Names | Typical Doses |
|------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Estradiol valerate (E2V) | Progynova, Estrace | 2–8 mg/day |
| Estradiol hemihydrate | Estrofem, Zumenon | 2–8 mg/day |
| Micronised estradiol | Various generic | 2–8 mg/day |

## How It Works

When swallowed, oral estradiol passes through the digestive system to the liver (first-pass metabolism). The liver converts much of it to **estrone (E1)**, a significantly weaker estrogen. This means:

- Oral estradiol produces a **higher E1:E2 ratio** compared to other routes
- Higher doses are needed to achieve target E2 levels
- Liver metabolism increases production of clotting factors (VTE risk)

::: warning First-pass metabolism
The liver converts approximately 80% of oral estradiol to estrone on first pass. This is why oral estrogen requires higher doses and carries a higher VTE risk compared to transdermal or injectable routes. Estrone is about 4% as potent as estradiol at estrogen receptors.
:::

## Dosing Guidelines

| Level | Dose |
|-------|------|
| **Starting dose** | 2–4 mg/day |
| **Typical maintenance** | 4–6 mg/day |
| **High dose** | 6–8 mg/day |
| **Dosing schedule** | Split into 2 doses (morning and evening) for more stable levels |

### Dosing Tips

- Split your daily dose into two equal portions (e.g., 2 mg morning, 2 mg evening)
- Taking with food may reduce nausea for some people
- Consistency matters — take at roughly the same times each day

## Side Effects

| Side Effect | Notes |
|-------------|-------|
| Nausea | Common in first few weeks; usually resolves |
| Headaches | Can occur with dose changes |
| Mood changes | Possible mood swings in early weeks |
| **VTE risk** | Increased risk of blood clots (see below) |
| Elevated triglycerides | Oral route raises triglycerides more than transdermal |
| Gallbladder disease | Increased risk; estrogen promotes gallstone formation |

::: danger VTE (Blood Clot) Risk
Oral estrogen increases the risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE) — blood clots in the legs (DVT) or lungs (PE). This is due to first-pass liver metabolism increasing clotting factors, particularly Factor IX and XI, and increasing APC resistance.

**Risk factors that amplify VTE risk:**
- Age over 40–45
- Smoking
- Obesity (BMI > 30)
- Personal or family history of blood clots
- Inherited clotting disorders (Factor V Leiden, etc.)
- Prolonged immobility

**If you have any of these risk factors, strongly consider transdermal or injectable estradiol instead.**
:::

## Monitoring

| Test | Frequency | Notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| E2 (estradiol) | Every 3 months until stable | Draw blood at trough (just before next dose) |
| T (testosterone) | Every 3 months | Target < 50 ng/dL |
| Lipid panel | Annually | Oral E2 can raise triglycerides |
| Liver function | Annually | ALT, AST |
| Blood pressure | Every visit | Estrogen can raise BP in some individuals |

## Pros vs Cons

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| Widely available prescribed | High E1:E2 ratio (low potency) |
| Easy to dose and adjust | High VTE risk |
| Familiar to doctors | Liver metabolism affects clotting |
| No needles | Daily dosing (can miss doses) |
| Inexpensive | Can raise triglycerides |
| Good initial option | Higher doses needed vs other routes |

## Who Should Avoid Oral Estrogen

- People over 40 (especially over 45)
- Smokers
- Anyone with personal/family history of blood clots
- Anyone with known clotting disorder
- People with high triglycerides or liver disease
- People with migraine with aura (estrogen can worsen)
