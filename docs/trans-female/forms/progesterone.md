# Progesterone

## Overview

Progesterone is a naturally occurring female hormone that some trans women add to their HRT regimen. While not strictly necessary for feminisation, many report benefits for breast maturation, libido, mood, and sleep.

| Property | Details |
|----------|---------|
| Drug class | Progestogen |
| Mechanism | Binds progesterone receptors; may support breast maturation |
| Half-life | 16–20 hours (oral) |
| Typical dose | 100–200 mg/day |
| Common brands | Utrogestan, Prometrium |

## Potential Benefits

| Benefit | Evidence Level | Notes |
|---------|---------------|-------|
| Breast maturation (Tanner 4–5) | Anecdotal / limited | May help with breast rounding and fullness |
| Increased libido | Anecdotal | Many report increased sexual desire |
| Improved sleep | Anecdotal | Calming effect for some |
| Mood improvement | Mixed | Some feel better; others feel worse |
| Reduced anxiety | Anecdotal | Calming effect reported |

::: info The evidence for progesterone
The evidence for progesterone's benefits in trans women is **largely anecdotal**. Clinical trials are limited and results are mixed. Some trans women report significant benefits; others report no effect or negative side effects. It is worth trying if you're curious, but not essential.
:::

## Timing

Most clinicians who prescribe progesterone recommend:

- **Wait 6–12 months** after starting estrogen before adding progesterone
- This allows baseline breast development to establish first
- Starting too early may stunt breast growth (theoretical concern, not proven)

## Dosing

| Level | Dose | Route | Notes |
|-------|------|-------|-------|
| Starting | 100 mg/day | Oral or rectal | Assess tolerance |
| Typical | 200 mg/day | Oral or rectal | Split 100 mg AM + 100 mg PM if needed |
| Maximum | 300 mg/day | Rarely needed |

### Routes of Administration

| Route | Bioavailability | Pros | Cons |
|-------|-----------------|------|------|
| **Oral** | ~10% | Convenient | First-pass liver metabolism; drowsiness |
| **Rectal** | ~25% | Better absorption, less drowsiness | Less convenient |
| **Sublingual** | Varies | Avoids first-pass | Difficult (pills crush easily) |

::: tip Rectal administration
Many trans women prefer rectal administration (inserting the capsule like a suppository) because:
- Higher bioavailability
- Fewer sedative effects
- Less liver metabolism

To use rectally: insert the capsule about 1–2 inches before bed. The capsule shell dissolves and the progesterone is absorbed.
:::

## Side Effects

| Side Effect | Frequency | Notes |
|-------------|-----------|-------|
| Drowsiness/sedation | Very common (oral) | Take at bedtime |
| Mood swings | Some | Can cause depression in susceptible people |
| Weight gain | Some | Progestogenic effect |
| Breast tenderness | Common | Similar to menstrual breast pain |
| Bloating | Some | Temporary |
| Headaches | Occasional | |

::: warning Progesterone can worsen depression
If you have a history of depression, monitor your mood carefully when starting progesterone. Some people experience significant mood decline. Stop and consult a provider if this happens.
:::

## What Form of Progesterone to Use

| Type | Recommendation | Notes |
|------|---------------|-------|
| **Micronised progesterone** | ✅ **Recommended** | Bioidentical; safest option |
| Medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA, Provera) | ❌ **Not recommended** | Synthetic progestin; less favourable side effect profile |
| Dydrogesterone | ⚠️ Limited data | Synthetic progestin; less studied in trans women |
| Norethisterone | ❌ Not recommended | Synthetic; androgenic side effects |

## Does Progesterone Actually Help Breast Growth?

This is one of the most debated topics in trans feminine HRT.

- **Theory:** Progesterone is involved in breast maturation (alveolar development) in cisgender women during puberty
- **Anecdotal evidence:** Many trans women report breast rounding and fullness after adding progesterone
- **Scientific evidence:** Limited. A few small studies suggest possible benefit; larger trials have not been done
- **Verdict:** Worth trying, but not guaranteed. If it works for you, you'll likely notice within 2–3 months
