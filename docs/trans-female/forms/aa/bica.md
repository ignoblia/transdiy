# Bicalutamide

## Overview

Bicalutamide is a non-steroidal androgen receptor blocker. Unlike spironolactone or CPA, it does **not** lower testosterone levels — instead, it blocks testosterone from binding to androgen receptors throughout the body.

| Property | Details |
|----------|---------|
| Drug class | Non-steroidal androgen receptor antagonist |
| Mechanism | Competitively binds androgen receptors, blocking T and DHT |
| Half-life | 5–7 days (very long) |
| Typical dose | 25–50 mg/day |
| Frequency | Once daily |

## How It Works

Bicalutamide binds to androgen receptors with high affinity, preventing testosterone and DHT from activating them. Because it does not suppress testosterone production, your total T levels may actually **rise** (the body compensates by producing more), but the testosterone cannot bind to receptors and therefore has no masculinising effect.

## Dosing

| Level | Dose | Notes |
|-------|------|-------|
| Starting | 25 mg/day | May be sufficient for many |
| Typical | 50 mg/day | Most common dose |
| High | 50 mg/day | Exceeding 50 mg increases hepatotoxicity risk without clear additional benefit |

## Pros

| Benefit | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Low side effect burden** | No diuretic effects, no potassium issues |
| **No hyperkalemia risk** | Safe with ACE inhibitors / NSAIDs |
| **Once-daily dosing** | Long half-life allows convenient dosing |
| **Effective for hair preservation** | Particularly good for scalp hair |
| **Good for those who can't tolerate spiro** | Low hassle alternative |
| **Does not cause depression** | Unlike CPA in some people |

## Side Effects

| Side Effect | Frequency | Notes |
|-------------|-----------|-------|
| Breast tenderness | Common | Expected androgen blockade effect |
| Hot flashes | Some | Due to high free T levels |
| Fatigue | Occasional | Usually resolves |
| Mild liver enzyme elevation | Monitor | Generally not clinically significant |
| **Hepatotoxicity** | **Rare** | Requires liver monitoring |

::: warning Liver Function Monitoring
Bicalutamide carries a small risk of hepatotoxicity (liver injury). While rare at HRT doses, adhere to the following monitoring schedule:

| Time       | Test       | Action                              |
| ---------- | ---------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Baseline   | ALT, AST   | Establish baseline                  |
| 1 month    | ALT, AST   | Early detection of rare reaction    |
| 3 months   | ALT, AST   | Confirm safety                      |
| 6 months   | ALT, AST   | Ongoing monitoring                  |
| Annually   | ALT, AST   | Long-term safety                    |

If you develop jaundice (yellow skin/eyes), dark urine, or severe fatigue, stop bicalutamide and seek medical attention immediately.
:::

## Comparison

| Aspect | Bicalutamide | Spironolactone | CPA |
|--------|-------------|---------------|-----|
| Receptor blocker? | Yes (strong) | Yes (weak) | No (progestogen) |
| Lowers T? | No (may raise) | Mildly | Yes (strongly) |
| Liver risk | Rare | No | Possible |
| Meningioma risk | No | No | Yes |
| Potassium issues | No | Yes | No |
| Half-life | 5–7 days | 1.4 hours | 38 hours |

## Who Should Consider Bicalutamide

- People who get side effects from spironolactone
- Those who cannot take CPA (meningioma risk, depression)
- People on medications that interact with spironolactone
- Those who want minimal side effects
