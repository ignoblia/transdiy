# Blood Testing Guide

## Why Blood Tests Are Essential

Blood tests are the single most important harm reduction practice for DIY HRT. Without them, you are **dosing blind**  -  you have no way of knowing whether your levels are in range, too low (wasting money and getting no results), or too high (increasing health risks).

| Benefit | Why It Matters |
|---------|----------------|
| **Confirm your dose works** | Are your levels in target range? |
| **Detect problems early** | Liver strain, high haematocrit, high prolactin  -  all treatable if caught early. |
| **Adjust with evidence** | Changes should be based on data, not guesses. |
| **Peace of mind** | Knowing your levels reduces anxiety about DIY. |

## What to Test

### Baseline (Before Starting HRT)

| Test | What It Shows |
|------|---------------|
| Estradiol (E2) | Baseline oestrogen level |
| Testosterone (total and free) | Baseline T level |
| LH, FSH | HPG axis function (pituitary response) |
| SHBG | Hormone-binding protein |
| CBC (complete blood count) | Red/white blood cells, platelets |
| Comprehensive metabolic panel | Liver function (ALT, AST), kidney function (creatinine), electrolytes |
| Lipid panel | Cholesterol, triglycerides |
| Prolactin | Baseline (especially important if using CPA) |

### On HRT  -  First Year (Every 3 Months)

| Test | Target (Trans Fem) | Target (Trans Masc) |
|------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **E2** | 100–200 pg/mL | < 50 pg/mL |
| **Total T** | < 50 ng/dL | 300–1,000 ng/dL |
| **Free T** |  -  | Mid-to-upper normal range |
| **SHBG** | 40–120 nmol/L (normal; > 150 signals excess binding) | 10–50 nmol/L |
| **CBC** | Normal | HCT < 54% |
| **CMP** | Normal | Normal |
| **Prolactin** | Normal (< 25 ng/mL) | N/A |
| **Lipid panel** | Monitor changes | Monitor changes |

### After Stabilisation (Every 6–12 Months)

Same panel as above, plus any additional markers your specific regimen requires (e.g. prolactin for CPA users, haematocrit for T users).

## Tips for Accurate Results

| Tip | Detail |
|-----|--------|
| **Test at trough** | The lowest point in your dosing cycle  -  just before your next dose for injections, first thing in the morning for transdermal/oral. |
| **Use the same lab** | Different labs use different assays; consistency gives you comparable results over time. |
| **Avoid biotin** | Stop biotin-containing supplements 3–5 days before testing  -  it skews immunoassay results (LC-MS/MS is not affected by biotin, so if your lab uses LC-MS/MS this precaution is unnecessary). |
| **Be consistent with timing** | Always test at the same point in your cycle for meaningful comparisons. |
| **Stay hydrated** | Dehydration can concentrate blood and skew results. |
| **Don't rush** | If you change dose, wait 4–6 weeks before testing to reach steady state. |

## Key Markers Explained

### For Trans Women / Trans Fem People

| Marker | Target | If Too Low | If Too High |
|--------|--------|------------|-------------|
| **E2** | 100–200 pg/mL | Poor feminisation, low mood, hot flushes | VTE risk, mood changes, high SHBG (which binds E2 and reduces its effectiveness) |
| **Total T** | < 50 ng/dL | (N/A)  -  lower is generally the goal | Masculinisation continues, androgenic side effects |
| **E1 (Estrone)** | Not routinely needed | Elevated with oral E2 due to first-pass metabolism | WPATH SOC8 states estrone/estradiol ratios need not be measured. Recent evidence does not support the claim that high E1:E2 ratios impair feminisation. Switching to parenteral routes (injections, patches, gel) avoids high E1 levels. |
| **SHBG** | 40–120 nmol/L | Below 40: free E2 may be higher than expected | > 150 nmol/L binds too much E2, reducing effectiveness  -  reduce E2 dose if high |
| **Prolactin** | < 25 ng/mL | Normal | CPA side effect; risk of prolactinoma if persistently > 100 ng/mL |
| **HCT** | < 48% | Normal | Polycythaemia risk  -  rare on E alone |

### For Trans Men / Trans Masc People

| Marker | Target | If Too Low | If Too High |
|--------|--------|------------|-------------|
| **Total T** | 300–1,000 ng/dL | Poor masculinisation, fatigue, low libido | Polycythaemia, irritability/aggression, acne, scalp hair loss, high E2 (from aromatisation) |
| **Free T** | Mid-to-upper normal range | Symptoms of low T despite normal total T | Androgenic side effects  -  check SHBG if this is disproportionate |
| **E2** | < 50 pg/mL | (N/A) | High T → aromatisation to E2 → possible oestrogenic effects (mood changes, water retention) |
| **HCT** | < 54% | Normal | Polycythaemia  -  donate blood if high; reduce T dose if persistent |
| **SHBG** | 10–50 nmol/L | May cause high free T symptoms | Lowers free T availability |

## Understanding Your Results

### E2 (Estradiol)  -  Trans Fem Special Considerations

| Scenario | Likely Cause | Action |
|----------|-------------|--------|
| E2 low despite moderate dose | Oral E2 has low bioavailability; high SHBG binding; dose too low | Increase dose or switch to parenteral route (sublingual, transdermal, injection) |
| E2 high despite moderate dose | Injectable E2 produces high peaks; sensitive to E2 | Reduce dose; check trough levels only |
| E2 low and SHBG high | Too much E2 causing SHBG to rise, which then binds E2 | Reduce E2 dose  -  paradoxically, less E2 can free up more bioavailable E2 |
| E1 much higher than E2 | Taking oral oestradiol  -  high first-pass conversion | Switch to sublingual/buccal, transdermal, or injectable if desired. Note: WPATH SOC8 states E1:E2 ratio need not be measured as recent evidence does not support it affecting outcomes. |

### T (Testosterone)  -  Trans Masc Special Considerations

| Scenario | Likely Cause | Action |
|----------|-------------|--------|
| T low despite standard dose | Poor absorption (gel); wrong injection frequency | Switch to injections or increase gel dose; shorten injection interval |
| T high despite standard dose | Genetics; injecting too frequently for your ester | Reduce dose or lengthen injection interval |
| T peak too high, trough too low | Injection interval too long for your ester | Divide dose (e.g. once weekly -> twice weekly) |
| HCT rising | T stimulating erythropoiesis | Donate blood; if persistent, reduce T dose |

## SHBG Explained

Sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) is a protein produced by the liver that binds to oestradiol and testosterone, making them unavailable to tissues. Only the unbound (free) fraction is biologically active.

### How HRT Affects SHBG

| Regimen | Effect on SHBG | What It Means |
|---------|---------------|---------------|
| Oral oestradiol | Raises SHBG significantly | High first-pass liver metabolism stimulates SHBG production |
| Injectable oestradiol | Raises SHBG moderately | Less liver impact than oral |
| Transdermal oestradiol | Raises SHBG minimally | Lowest liver impact |
| Testosterone therapy | Lowers SHBG | T suppresses SHBG production |
| CPA (cyproterone acetate) | May raise SHBG | Adds to oestrogen's effect |

### Interpreting SHBG

| SHBG Level | What It Suggests |
|------------|-----------------|
| < 40 nmol/L (trans fem) | Low SHBG can mean free E2 is higher than expected for a given total E2. May cause androgenic symptoms if T is not fully suppressed. |
| 40-120 nmol/L (trans fem) | Normal range. Total E2 roughly reflects free E2. |
| > 150 nmol/L (trans fem) | High SHBG binds too much E2. You may need a higher total E2 to achieve adequate free E2. Paradoxically, reducing the E2 dose can lower SHBG and free up more bioavailable E2. |
| < 10 nmol/L (trans masc) | Low SHBG means free T is high relative to total T. Can cause androgenic side effects even if total T is in range. |
| 10-50 nmol/L (trans masc) | Normal range. |
| > 50 nmol/L (trans masc) | Uncommon on T therapy (T lowers SHBG). May indicate thyroid issue or other condition. |

## Assay Types: LC-MS/MS vs Immunoassay

Not all lab tests are equally accurate. The type of assay used matters, especially at low or high hormone concentrations.

| Assay Type | How It Works | Accuracy | Best For |
|-------------|-------------|----------|----------|
| **LC-MS/MS** (liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry) | Separates molecules by mass and charge | Gold standard - highly accurate at all levels | Low E2 (< 50 pg/mL), low T (< 50 ng/dL), free T by equilibrium dialysis |
| **Immunoassay** (CLIA, ECLIA, RIA) | Uses antibodies to detect hormones | Less accurate at extremes; can overestimate at low levels | Routine monitoring at moderate-to-high levels; cheaper |

### Which Assay Should You Get?

- **Trans women**: If you are testing E2, immunoassay is usually fine above 50 pg/mL. For T suppression (< 50 ng/dL), LC-MS/MS is more reliable - immunoassay can overestimate low T.
- **Trans men**: For total T, immunoassay is usually adequate in the 300-1000 ng/dL range. For free T, ask for equilibrium dialysis (the most accurate method).
- **If your levels do not match your symptoms**: Consider retesting with LC-MS/MS. Immunoassay errors are a common cause of confusing results.

### Checking Your Lab Report

Look at your lab report for method notation. LC-MS/MS is often labelled as "LC/MS/MS", "Mass Spectrometry", or "Tandem Mass Spec". Immunoassay may be labelled as "ECLIA", "CLIA", "Chemiluminescent", or simply "Immunoassay".

## Free vs Bound Hormones

Standard blood tests measure **total** hormone levels (bound + free). But only the **free** fraction is biologically active.

| Marker | What It Measures | When to Check |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Total T** | All testosterone (bound to SHBG + bound to albumin + free) | Standard monitoring |
| **Free T** | Testosterone not bound to any protein | If total T seems inconsistent with symptoms (e.g. low T symptoms despite normal total T) |
| **Free T by equilibrium dialysis** | Gold standard for free T | Most accurate method |
| **Free androgen index (FAI)** | Calculated from total T and SHBG | Rough estimate only - less accurate than direct measurement |
| **Bioavailable T** | Free T + albumin-bound T (albumin binding is weak, so this T is still active) | Sometimes calculated instead of free T |

**Rule of thumb**: If your SHBG is within the normal range, total hormone levels are a reasonable guide. If SHBG is high or low, free hormone levels give a truer picture.

## Prolactin Management

Prolactin is a pituitary hormone that can rise on oestrogen therapy, especially when combined with cyproterone acetate (CPA).

### Prolactin Levels and Action

| Level | Risk | Action |
|-------|------|--------|
| < 25 ng/mL | Normal | No action needed |
| 25-50 ng/mL | Mild elevation | Monitor. Common on E + CPA. Recheck in 3 months. |
| 50-100 ng/mL | Moderate elevation | Consider reducing CPA dose or switching AA. Rule out other causes (stress, sleep, other meds). |
| > 100 ng/mL | Significant elevation | Stop CPA. Switch to bicalutamide or monotherapy. Consider MRI to rule out prolactinoma. |
| > 200 ng/mL | High concern | Likely prolactinoma. Needs endocrinology workup and MRI. |

### Reducing Prolactin

1. **Reduce or stop CPA** - The most common cause in trans women. CPA at 12.5 mg/day or less is safer than 25-50 mg/day.
2. **Switch to bicalutamide** - Bicalutamide does not raise prolactin.
3. **Switch to oestrogen monotherapy** - High enough E2 levels suppress T without an AA.
4. **Check for other causes** - Stress, sleep deprivation, chest wall stimulation, hypothyroidism, and certain medications (antipsychotics, antidepressants) can all raise prolactin.

## Home Finger-Prick vs Venous Blood Draw

Many direct-to-consumer services offer home finger-prick kits. These are convenient but have limitations compared to venous blood draw.

| Aspect | Finger-Prick (Capillary) | Venous Draw |
|--------|-------------------------|-------------|
| **Sample volume** | Very small (a few drops) | Larger (several mL) - can run many tests |
| **Accuracy** | Can be affected by squeezing, haemolysis, or contamination | More reliable, less prone to collection errors |
| **Number of tests** | Limited by volume | Can run a full panel from one draw |
| **Convenience** | High - do it at home, mail it in | Requires clinic visit |
| **Cost** | Usually lower | Usually higher |
| **Turnaround** | 3-7 days (includes shipping time) | 1-3 days |

**Recommendation**: Finger-prick tests are fine for routine monitoring of a few key markers (E2, T, SHBG) after you are stable. For your first test, a full panel, or if results seem off, use venous draw for accuracy.

### Using a Spreadsheet or App

A simple spreadsheet (Google Sheets, Excel) or a notes app works well. Plotting E2 and T values over time on a chart makes it easy to see whether your levels are trending up, down, or stable. Many community members share template spreadsheets on r/TransDIY.

## Troubleshooting Common Problems

| Problem | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| **GP refuses to test** | Switch to a private online service (Medichecks, Thriva, Private MD Labs). Some GPs respond better if you say you are "investigating symptoms" rather than monitoring DIY HRT. |
| **Results seem wrong** | Rule out lab error  -  retest at a different lab or at a different point in your cycle. Check if you took biotin recently. Consider assay type (immunoassay error is common at low T levels). |
| **Can't get trough timing right** | For injections, test immediately before your next shot. For gel, test in the morning before applying. For patches, test just before changing. |
| **Cost is prohibitive** | Check if your country has free sexual health clinics; use sliding-scale services; find community members who pool tests (buy one panel, share the cost). |
| **Different labs give different results** | Labs use different assays. Stick to one lab for consistency. If switching labs, do one test at both to calibrate. |
| **Doctor says your levels are "normal" but they are not in target range** | Many labs use cisgender reference ranges. Explain that you are on HRT and need transgender-specific targets. Bring the WPATH or Endocrine Society targets. |
