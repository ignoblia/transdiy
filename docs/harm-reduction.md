# Harm Reduction Overview

## What Is Harm Reduction?

Harm reduction is a set of practical strategies aimed at reducing the negative consequences of a behaviour while acknowledging that the behaviour may continue. It is **not** about endorsing or encouraging DIY HRT  -  it is about making it **safer** for those who choose to pursue it regardless.

Applied to DIY HRT, harm reduction means:

- **Acknowledging reality**  -  People will self-medicate regardless of legality, accessibility, or medical guidelines.
- **Providing actionable information**  -  Giving people the knowledge to minimise risks (e.g. proper dosing, sterile technique, source verification).
- **Meeting people where they are**  -  Not judging or gatekeeping, but offering evidence-based guidance.
- **Supporting informed decisions**  -  Helping individuals weigh benefits against risks so they can make their own choices.

> Harm reduction does not replace medical supervision  -  it fills the gap when medical care is inaccessible, unaffordable, or otherwise unavailable.

## 1. Blood Testing & Lab Work

Regular blood tests are the single most important harm reduction practice for DIY HRT. Without them, you are **dosing blind**.

### Recommended Schedule

| Phase | Frequency | Rationale |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| **Before starting** | Once | Establishes baseline hormone levels, liver/kidney function, and metabolic health. |
| **First year (titration)** | Every 3 months | Allows dose adjustments to reach target ranges safely. |
| **After stabilisation** | Every 6–12 months | Monitors long-term safety and detects issues early. |
| **After any dose change** | 6–8 weeks post-change | Confirms new dose achieves desired levels. |
| **If symptoms arise** | As needed | Investigates potential side effects or complications. |

> **Note on E1 (estrone):** WPATH SOC8 states that estrone/estradiol ratios need not be measured, as recent evidence does not support the claim that high E1:E2 ratios impair feminisation. Switching to parenteral routes (injections, patches, gel) avoids high E1 levels if using oral E2.

### Key Markers to Test

| Marker | What It Tells You | Target Range (Trans Fem) | Target Range (Trans Masc) |
|--------|-------------------|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| **E2 (Estradiol)** | Oestrogen level | 100–200 pg/mL (367–734 pmol/L) |  -  |
| **E1 (Estrone)** | Oestrogen metabolite (oral E2) | Not routinely needed — see note below |  -  |
| **Total T (Testosterone)** | Testosterone level | < 50 ng/dL (< 1.73 nmol/L) | 300–1000 ng/dL (10.4–34.7 nmol/L) |
| **Free T** | Active, unbound testosterone |  -  | Mid-upper normal range |
| **SHBG** | Hormone binding protein | 40–120 nmol/L | 10–50 nmol/L |
| **LH / FSH** | Pituitary function | Suppressed |  -  |
| **Prolactin** | Pituitary activity (monitor on CPA) | < 25 ng/mL (< 530 mIU/L) |  -  |
| **Haematocrit (HCT)** | Red blood cell concentration | < 48% | < 54% |
| **ALT / AST** | Liver function | Within lab range | Within lab range |
| **Creatinine / eGFR** | Kidney function | Within lab range | Within lab range |
| **Lipid panel** | Cholesterol (affected by hormones) | Monitor changes | Monitor changes |

### Where to Get Blood Tests Without a Prescription

- **🇺🇸 US:** Private lab companies like LabCorp and Quest Diagnostics allow direct ordering via third-party services (e.g. Walk-In Lab, Jason Health, Private MD). Prices range $40–150 depending on the panel.
- **🇬🇧 UK:** Medichecks, Randox Health, and Thriva offer finger-prick or venous blood test kits you can order online. GP requests are also possible (be honest about your HRT).
- **🇨🇦 Canada:** Private clinics (e.g. Lifelabs, Dynacare) accept self-referred blood work. Some provinces allow direct access without a doctor's referral.
- **🇦🇺 Australia:** GP-requested blood tests are the most common. Some online services (e.g. iMedical) offer limited self-requested tests.
- **🇪🇺 EU:** Availability varies. In many countries you can request tests through private labs or online services (e.g. GanzImmun in Germany, Cerba in France).
- **🌍 Other regions:** Search for "private blood test [country]" or check online forums for local recommendations.

## 2. Source & Product Verification

Buying hormones from unregulated sources carries risks  -  contamination, incorrect dosing, counterfeit products, and legal exposure.

### How to Verify a Source

| Step | What to Do |
|------|------------|
| **Check community reviews** | Search forums (Reddit, Discord, TransHarmReduction) for recent feedback. Look for **consistent positive reviews over 6+ months**, not just a handful of glowing posts. |
| **Ask for batch testing** | Reputable homebrewers and vendors provide **HPLC test results** from independent labs. A certificate of analysis (CoA) should show the exact concentration (mg/mL) and purity. |
| **Start small** | Order a single vial or a month's supply before committing to a larger purchase. |
| **Verify with blood tests** | After 4–6 weeks on a new product, check your levels. If they don't match expected results, the product may be under- or over-dosed. |
| **Check packaging** | Legitimate products arrive in sealed, labelled vials. Poorly labelled or unsealed packaging is a red flag. |
| **Use escrow / buyer protection** | When possible, pay via methods that offer dispute resolution (e.g. credit card, PayPal goods & services). Cryptocurrency is common but offers no buyer protection. |

### Red Flags

- Prices significantly below market average
- Refusal to provide batch test results
- Only accepting irreversible payment methods (e.g. unsolicited crypto, wire transfers)
- No community presence or history
- Pushy sales tactics or "limited time" pressure
- Vague or non-existent product labelling

### Homebrew vs. Pharmaceutical

| Aspect | Pharmaceutical | Homebrew |
|--------|---------------|----------|
| **Quality control** | Rigorous (GMP certified) | Variable  -  depends on the brewer |
| **Cost** | Higher (especially without insurance) | Typically much lower |
| **Availability** | Prescription required | No prescription needed |
| **Batch consistency** | High | Varies  -  batch testing is essential |
| **Legal risk** | Low (if prescribed) | Higher (importation, possession) |

> **Bottom line:** If you choose homebrew, **only buy from well-reviewed, batch-tested sources** and verify with blood work.

## 3. Safe Dosing Principles

### Start Low, Go Slow

| Principle | Explanation |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Start at the low end of the dose range** | See the [Dosing guides](/trans-female/dosing/starting) for starting doses. |
| **Wait for steady state** | Most injectables reach steady state after 4–6 weeks (5 half-lives). Do not redose before then. |
| **Change one variable at a time** | Never adjust dose, route, and frequency simultaneously  -  you won't know what caused the change. |
| **Base decisions on blood tests, not feelings** | How you feel is important, but subjective. Always correlate with lab results. |
| **Track everything** | Log dates, doses, injection sites, blood results, side effects, and subjective well-being. |

### Avoid Common Mistakes

- **Doubling up after a missed dose**  -  Just resume your next scheduled dose. Do not take extra to "catch up."
- **Stacking multiple routes**  -  Using patches + injections simultaneously without testing is dangerous.
- **Assuming "more is better"**  -  Supraphysiological levels increase risk of DVT, polycythaemia, and other complications without accelerating changes.
- **Mixing different products inconsistently**  -  Stick to one product at a time so you know what your levels correspond to.

## 4. Injection Safety & Infection Prevention

If you inject hormones, proper technique is critical. A full guide is at **[Injection Techniques & Safety](/injection-safety)**, but key harm reduction points are summarised here.

### Equipment Hygiene

| Item | Harm Reduction Practice |
|------|------------------------|
| **Needles** | Never share needles. Never reuse needles. Use once, dispose immediately. |
| **Syringes** | Single use only. Luer lock preferred for secure needle attachment. |
| **Alcohol swabs** | Wipe vial stopper for 10 seconds. Clean injection site in a circular motion, let dry. |
| **Sharps container** | Use a proper sharps bin or a thick, puncture-proof container (e.g. laundry detergent bottle). |
| **Hand hygiene** | Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water before handling any equipment. |

### Injection Site Rotation

Repeated injections in the same spot cause scar tissue, lipohypertrophy (lumps), and inconsistent absorption.

| Injection Type | Recommended Rotation |
|----------------|---------------------|
| **IM (intramuscular)** | Alternate between left and right ventrogluteal or thigh. Rotate sites each injection. |
| **SC (subcutaneous)** | Rotate across abdomen (leave 1–2 inches between sites), upper thighs, and upper arms. |

### Signs of Infection

Seek medical attention if you experience:

- Redness, swelling, or warmth at the injection site **worsening after 24–48 hours**
- Pus or unusual discharge
- Fever, chills, or malaise
- Increasing pain (not the usual injection soreness)

## 5. Recognising & Responding to Emergencies

Knowing the warning signs of serious complications can save your life.

### Venous Thromboembolism (DVT / PE)

Oestrogen increases clotting risk, particularly in the first year and at high doses.

| Condition | Symptoms | Action |
|-----------|----------|--------|
| **Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)** | Unilateral leg swelling, pain, warmth, redness | **Go to ER immediately.** Do not massage the leg. |
| **Pulmonary Embolism (PE)** | Sudden shortness of breath, chest pain (worse on deep breath), coughing up blood, rapid heart rate | **Call emergency services.** This is life-threatening. |

**Risk factors that amplify DVT risk:**
- Smoking (significantly increases risk)
- Obesity (BMI > 30)
- Age > 35 (especially > 40)
- Personal or family history of clots
- High-dose oral oestrogen (> 4 mg/day oral E2)
- Prolonged immobility (long flights, bed rest)
- Certain clotting disorders (Factor V Leiden, etc.)

### Polycythaemia (High Haematocrit)

More common with testosterone therapy.

| Symptoms | Blood Marker | Action |
|----------|-------------|--------|
| Headaches, dizziness, blurred vision, fatigue, shortness of breath, ruddy complexion | Haematocrit > 54% | Stop T temporarily. Donate blood (therapeutic phlebotomy). Reduce dose. Recheck in 4–6 weeks. |

### Meningioma (Cyproterone Acetate / CPA)

CPA carries a dose-dependent risk of meningioma (a usually benign brain tumour).

| Symptoms | Risk Profile | Action |
|----------|-------------|--------|
| Persistent headaches, vision changes (blurring, double vision), seizures, hearing loss, loss of smell | Cumulative dose > 3 g (equivalent to 3 years at 12.5 mg/day) | Discontinue CPA immediately. Switch to another AA (bicalutamide, GnRH agonist) or monotherapy. Get an MRI. |

### Severe Hyperkalaemia (Spironolactone)

Less common at low doses (< 100 mg/day), but possible, especially with dehydration or kidney issues.

| Symptoms | Action |
|----------|--------|
| Muscle weakness, fatigue, irregular heartbeat, nausea | Reduce or stop spiro. Increase water intake. Check potassium on next blood test. |

### Anaphylaxis / Allergic Reaction

Rare but possible with any injectable medication.

| Symptoms | Action |
|----------|--------|
| Hives, itching, swelling of face/lips/tongue, difficulty breathing, wheezing, dizziness | **Call emergency services immediately.** Use an EpiPen if available. |

## 6. Mental Health & Community Care

### Hormones and Mood

Hormone therapy can significantly affect your mental health  -  both positively (e.g. relief from dysphoria) and negatively (e.g. mood swings from unstable levels).

| Situation | What to Do |
|-----------|------------|
| **Estrogen causing low mood / depression** | Check if E2 levels are too high or too low. Consider switching from oral to injectable (avoid first-pass metabolism). Monitor prolactin if on CPA. |
| **Testosterone causing irritability / aggression** | Check total and free T levels. Consider a slightly lower dose or more frequent injections (smaller peaks). |
| **Dysphoria worsening despite HRT** | Review levels  -  suboptimal levels may not provide relief. Consider social transition support. |
| **Anxiety about DIY safety** | This is normal. Connect with community, educate yourself, and get regular blood tests to build confidence. |

### Online Communities & Peer Support

| Resource | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| **TransHarmReduction** [transharmreduction.org](https://www.transharmreduction.org/) | Evidence-based HRT safety guides and community forums. |
| **r/TransDIY** (Reddit) | One of the largest DIY HRT communities. Read the wiki, search past posts, ask informed questions. |
| **r/MtF** / **r/FtM** / **r/NonBinary** (Reddit) | Broader trans communities with daily discussion of HRT experiences. |
| **Discord servers** | Many real-time chat communities (e.g. TransDIY Discord, country-specific servers). Vet channels for quality moderation. |
| **Local trans support groups** | In-person or Zoom-based groups offer social support and local knowledge (e.g. which pharmacies are trans-friendly). |

### When to Seek Professional Help

- Suicidal ideation or self-harm  -  **Call a crisis line immediately** (see [Mental Health Resources](/community/mental-health)).
- Persistent low mood or anxiety that interferes with daily life.
- Suspected hormone-related mood disorder (e.g. severe PMS-like symptoms on progesterone).

## 7. Legal Safety & Operational Security

DIY HRT exists in a legal grey area in most countries. Understanding the risks and protecting yourself is an important part of harm reduction.

### Legal Status by Region

| Region | General Status | Key Considerations |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|
| **🇺🇸 US** | Grey  -  personal possession of unlabelled hormones is not explicitly illegal at the federal level, but importation of controlled substances is. | Schedule III drugs (T) face stricter controls than oestrogen. Personal use amounts are less likely to be prosecuted. |
| **🇬🇧 UK** | Grey  -  possession for personal use is not explicitly criminalised, but importation from outside the UK can be stopped by customs. | The MHRA can seize packages. In practice, small personal orders are usually ignored. |
| **🇨🇦 Canada** | Grey  -  Health Canada regulates steroids/hormones. Importation without a prescription is technically illegal but rarely enforced for small personal amounts. | Larger orders or repeat imports may trigger investigations. |
| **🇦🇺 Australia** | Strict  -  Testosterone is a controlled substance. Importing without a prescription is illegal and can lead to prosecution. | Oestrogen is less strictly controlled. Personal importation of up to 3 months' supply may be permitted under certain conditions. |
| **🇪🇺 EU** | Varies by country  -  most EU member states allow personal importation of unlicensed medicines for personal use (3-month supply limit in many). | Cross-border EU purchases are generally safer than from outside the EU. |
| **🌍 Other regions** | Varies widely  -  research local laws carefully. | When in doubt, consult a local legal aid organisation or trans community. |

### OpSec (Operational Security) Tips

| Area | Recommendation |
|------|----------------|
| **Payment** | Use cryptocurrency (e.g. Monero > Bitcoin for privacy). Avoid bank transfers or credit card payments to sources. |
| **Shipping** | Use a real name and address (fake names attract suspicion). Opt for discreet packaging when offered. Track your package. |
| **Communication** | Use encrypted messaging (Signal, Telegram) for sensitive conversations. Avoid discussing sources on public forums. |
| **Digital footprint** | Consider a separate email address for HRT-related accounts. Use a VPN when accessing sourcing forums. |
| **Home storage** | Store medication in a cool, dry place  -  not in plain sight. Keep it out of reach of children and visitors. |
| **Emergency plan** | If you are stopped at customs or questioned by authorities, **do not lie** but also do not volunteer information. Ask for a lawyer. |

## 8. Contingency Planning

### Medication Stockpiles

| Scenario | Recommended Buffer |
|----------|-------------------|
| **Lost / seized package** | 3 months' extra supply |
| **Vendor goes offline** | 3 months' extra supply |
| **Personal emergency (hospitalisation, etc.)** | 1 month's extra supply accessible to a trusted person |
| **Supply chain disruption** | 6 months' extra supply (if feasible) |

### If You Run Out

1. **Do not panic**  -  a few days to weeks off HRT is uncomfortable but not dangerous.
2. **Reduce your dose before you run out**  -  a low maintenance dose uses less product and maintains some levels.
3. **Contact community sources**  -  someone may have a spare vial they can sell or give you.
4. **Seek a bridge prescription**  -  some GPs or informed consent clinics will provide a short-term prescription if you explain your situation honestly.
5. **Restart safely**  -  if you restart after a break, retake baseline blood tests and resume at the starting dose, not your previous maintenance dose.

### Medical Emergencies

- **Wear a medical alert card or note** in your wallet stating that you are on HRT (including specific medications and doses). This helps ER doctors avoid misdiagnosis.
- **Tell your healthcare provider** if you can. Most confidentiality laws protect your privacy.
- **If you cannot or will not disclose DIY status**, at least know the signs of complications so you can seek help early.

## 9. Traveling with HRT

See the **[Traveling with HRT FAQ](/trans-female/faq/travel)** for a detailed guide. Key harm reduction points:

| Situation | Recommendation |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Domestic travel** | Keep medication in original labelled packaging (or pharmacy labels if possible). Carry a reasonable amount for your trip length. |
| **International travel** | Check the legal status of your medication in **both** the destination country and any transit countries. Carry a copy of any relevant prescriptions or a doctor's letter. |
| **Injectables in carry-on** | Syringes and needles are allowed in carry-on luggage in most countries if accompanied by medication. Check specific airline and country rules. |
| **Never check HRT in hold luggage** | Temperature extremes in cargo holds can degrade medication. Also risk of loss or theft. |

## 10. Drug Interactions & Contraindications

### Medications That Interact with HRT

| Medication / Substance | Interaction with E | Interaction with T |
|------------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Smoking** | Significantly increases DVT risk (especially with oral E) | Increases cardiovascular risk |
| **Alcohol** | May increase liver strain (especially with oral E) | May increase liver strain |
| **Anticoagulants (warfarin, apixaban)** | E potentiates anticoagulant effect  -  increased bleeding risk |  -  |
| **Carbamazepine, phenytoin, rifampicin** | Reduces E effectiveness (enzyme induction) | May reduce T effectiveness |
| **Corticosteroids** | Increased fluid retention risk | Increased fluid retention risk |
| **Insulin / diabetes medications** | E may affect insulin sensitivity  -  monitor blood sugar | T may improve insulin sensitivity |
| **St. John's Wort** | Reduces E effectiveness (enzyme induction) |  -  |

### Contraindications (Absolute / Relative)

| Condition | E | T |
|-----------|-----|-----|
| **History of DVT / PE** | Contraindicated (unless using transdermal + anticoagulation) | Caution |
| **Breast cancer (personal history)** | Contraindicated (unless specialist-approved) |  -  |
| **Prostate cancer** |  -  | Contraindicated |
| **Severe liver disease** | Oral E contraindicated (transdermal may be acceptable) | Caution |
| **Polycythaemia (HCT > 54%)** |  -  | Contraindicated until resolved |
| **Migraine with aura** | Oral E increases stroke risk (transdermal safer) |  -  |

> **Always check with a healthcare provider** if you have any of these conditions. This list is not exhaustive.

## 11. Medication Storage & Integrity

Proper storage keeps your medication effective and safe. Using degraded or contaminated medication is a preventable harm.

### Storage Conditions by Formulation

| Formulation | Temperature | Light | Other |
|-------------|-------------|-------|-------|
| Injectable vials (oil-based) | 15-25 degrees C (room temp) | Away from direct light | Do not refrigerate - cold oil is thicker and harder to inject |
| Injectable vials (water-based) | 15-25 degrees C (room temp) | Away from direct light | Some may be refrigerated; check product leaflet |
| Gel / pump packs | 15-25 degrees C (room temp) | Away from direct light | Keep pump tightly closed |
| Patches | Below 25 degrees C | In original sealed pouch | Do not cut patches |
| Pills (oral / sublingual) | 15-25 degrees C (room temp) | Away from direct light, moisture | Keep in original bottle with desiccant if provided |

### Checking for Contamination

Before each use, inspect your medication:

| Sign | What It Means | Action |
|------|---------------|--------|
| Cloudiness (in oil) | Water contamination or bacterial growth | Do not use. Discard the vial. |
| Visible particles or floating material | Contamination or product degradation | Do not use. Discard the vial. |
| Colour change | Oxidation or degradation | Do not use if colour is noticeably different from when new. |
| Broken seal or cap | Tampering or sterility breach | Do not use. |
| Cracks in glass vial | Sterility breach | Do not use. Transfer remaining medication to a new sterile vial only if you have the equipment. |
| Expired medication | Reduced potency or increased degradation products | Discard. Do not use expired medication. |

### Shelf Life

- **Pharmaceutical vials**: Unopened, they last until the expiration date printed on the box (typically 2-3 years from manufacture).
- **Homebrew vials**: Vary by brewer. Most homebrewers state a 1-2 year shelf life if stored properly. Follow the brewer's guidance.
- **After opening**: A multidose vial should be used within 28 days of first puncture (pharmaceutical standard). Some homebrewers state longer. When in doubt, 28 days is a safe rule.
- **Gel pumps**: Use within 6 months of opening or by the expiration date, whichever comes first.
- **Patches**: Use by the expiration date on the pouch. Do not use if the pouch seal is broken.

### What to Do with Expired or Suspect Medication

1. Do not flush down the toilet or pour down the sink (environmental contamination).
2. Mix with coffee grounds, cat litter, or dirt in a sealed bag to make it unappealing.
3. Dispose of in household trash (for small amounts). For large amounts, check for local medication take-back programs.
4. Report suspected contamination to the seller and to the community (warn others).

## 12. Allergic Reactions & Adverse Effects

Allergic reactions to hormone preparations are rare but possible. The allergen is often the carrier oil (cottonseed, sesame, MCT, castor, grapeseed) rather than the hormone itself.

### Types of Reactions

| Type | Symptoms | Onset | Action |
|------|----------|-------|--------|
| Irritation at injection site | Redness, itching, mild swelling at injection site only | Hours after injection | Usually resolves on its own. Try a different carrier oil next time. |
| Delayed-type hypersensitivity | Localised rash, hives, swelling that spreads beyond injection site | 12-48 hours after injection | Antihistamine (cetirizine, loratadine). Consider switching carrier oil. |
| Immediate hypersensitivity | Hives, widespread itching, flushing | Minutes to 1 hour after injection | Antihistamine. If symptoms worsen, seek medical attention. |
| Anaphylaxis | Difficulty breathing, swelling of face/lips/tongue, wheezing, dizziness, rapid heart rate | Minutes after injection | **Call emergency services immediately.** Use EpiPen if available. Lie flat, raise legs. |

### Switching Carrier Oils

If you suspect an allergy to the carrier oil:

1. Identify the oil used in your current vial (ask your supplier or check the product information).
2. Find a version of the same hormone in a different carrier oil. Common alternatives:
   - MCT oil (thin, low allergy risk)
   - Grapeseed oil (common in European homebrew)
   - Sesame oil (used in some pharmaceutical T enanthate)
   - Cottonseed oil (used in some pharmaceutical T cypionate)
   - Castor oil (used in pharmaceutical EV)
   - Ethyl oleate (thinner than most oils, low viscosity)
3. Start with a small test dose of the new oil (0.1 mL) and wait 24-48 hours to observe for reactions before using a full dose.

### When to Seek Emergency Care

Call emergency services (or have someone drive you to an ER) if you experience:
- Difficulty breathing, wheezing, or throat tightness
- Swelling of the face, lips, tongue, or throat
- Severe hives covering large areas of the body
- Dizziness, fainting, or rapid heart rate
- Nausea/vomiting after injection (can be a sign of anaphylaxis)
