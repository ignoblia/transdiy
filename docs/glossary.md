# Glossary of Terms

## A

**AA** — Anti-androgen. A medication that blocks or reduces the effects of testosterone.

**AFAB** — Assigned Female At Birth.

**ALT / AST** — Alanine transaminase and aspartate transaminase. Liver enzymes measured in a blood test to monitor liver function (see **LFT**).

**AMAB** — Assigned Male At Birth.

**Androgen** — A hormone that promotes masculine characteristics, e.g., testosterone and DHT.

**Aromatase** — An enzyme that converts testosterone into estradiol.

**Aromatase inhibitor** — A medication (anastrozole, letrozole) that blocks aromatase, reducing conversion of T to E2.

## B

**Bica / Bicalutamide** — A non-steroidal androgen receptor blocker used as an anti-androgen in feminising HRT.

**Bioavailability** — The fraction of a drug that reaches systemic circulation unchanged. Routes with higher bioavailability (injections, sublingual) require lower doses than oral.

**Bioidentical** — Chemically identical to hormones naturally produced by the human body (e.g., 17β-estradiol, bioidentical progesterone). Distinct from synthetic analogues like ethinylestradiol.

**Bottom growth** — Clitoral enlargement caused by testosterone therapy.

**Buccal** — Absorbed through the gum or cheek lining (e.g., buccal testosterone tablets).

**Buserelin** — A GnRH agonist used as an anti-androgen, typically administered as a nasal spray.

## C

**Carrier oil** — The oil (MCT, grapeseed, sesame, etc.) in which injectable hormones are dissolved.

**CBC** — Complete blood count. A blood test measuring red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets.

**Cisgender** — A person whose gender identity aligns with the sex they were assigned at birth.

**CMP** — Comprehensive metabolic panel. A blood test measuring liver enzymes, kidney function, and electrolytes.

**Contraindication** — A condition or factor that makes a particular treatment inadvisable.

**CPA / Cyproterone acetate** — A progestogenic anti-androgen that suppresses testosterone production; used mainly outside the US.

**Counterfeit** — Fake or adulterated medication, often under-dosed, over-dosed, or contaminated.

**Cypionate** — An ester (e.g., testosterone cypionate, estradiol cypionate) that slows hormone release, allowing less frequent dosing.

## D

**DHT** — Dihydrotestosterone. A potent androgen converted from testosterone; linked to hair loss and bottom growth.

**DIY** — Do-It-Yourself. Obtaining and managing HRT without a prescription or medical supervision.

**DVT** — Deep vein thrombosis. A blood clot in a deep vein, usually in the leg; can travel to the lungs (see **PE**).

## E

**E1 / Estrone** — A weak estrogen produced mainly by the liver from oral estradiol. High E1:E2 ratios are a known effect of oral administration.

**E2 / Estradiol** — The primary active form of estrogen in the body; also called 17β-estradiol. This is the main hormone used in feminising HRT.

**EC** — Estradiol cypionate. An injectable estradiol ester with a half-life of 5–7 days.

**EEn** — Estradiol enanthate. An injectable estradiol ester with a half-life of 7–10 days.

**Enanthate** — An ester (e.g., estradiol enanthate, testosterone enanthate) that slows hormone release for weekly or longer dosing intervals.

**Endocrine Society** — A professional medical organisation whose 2017 clinical practice guideline is the most widely cited reference for gender-affirming hormone therapy.

**Estrogen** — A class of hormones that promote feminine characteristics. The primary estrogen in HRT is 17β-estradiol (see **E2**).

**EV** — Estradiol valerate. An injectable estradiol ester with a half-life of 4–5 days (dosed every 5 days).

## F

**Filter / 0.22 μm syringe filter** — A sterile single-use filter that removes bacteria, fungal spores, and particulates from injectable solutions. **Mandatory for homebrew injectables**; not needed for sealed pharmaceutical vials.

**First-pass metabolism** — The processing of an orally ingested drug by the liver before it reaches systemic circulation. Oral estrogen undergoes extensive first-pass, increasing VTE risk and producing unfavourable E1:E2 ratios. Sublingual, transdermal, and injectable routes bypass first-pass.

**Free T** — Testosterone not bound to SHBG; the biologically active fraction of total testosterone.

**FSH** — Follicle-stimulating hormone. Part of the HPG axis; suppressed by exogenous sex hormones.

## G

**GAHT** — Gender-Affirming Hormone Therapy (also called HRT). The use of hormones to align secondary sex characteristics with gender identity.

**GnRH** — Gonadotropin-releasing hormone. The hypothalamic hormone that controls LH and FSH release.

**GnRH agonist / Puberty blocker** — A medication (leuprolide, goserelin, buserelin) that suppresses LH and FSH by overstimulating the pituitary, effectively shutting down gonadal hormone production. Used in adolescents and adults.

## H

**HCT / Hematocrit** — The percentage of red blood cells in blood. Monitored in testosterone therapy because elevated T can raise HCT (see **Polycythemia**).

**Hepatotoxicity** — Liver toxicity. A rare but serious risk of some medications used in HRT (bicalutamide, CPA, oral estrogen at very high doses).

**Homebrew** — Hormone medications (typically injectable estradiol) made by community members rather than pharmaceutical companies. Requires additional safety precautions including 0.22 μm filtration.

**Hormone** — A chemical messenger produced by endocrine glands that travels through the bloodstream to regulate bodily functions (growth, metabolism, reproduction, etc.).

**HPG axis** — Hypothalamus–Pituitary–Gonadal axis. The feedback loop that controls sex hormone production. HRT works by altering signals in this axis.

**HPLC** — High-performance liquid chromatography. A laboratory method used to verify the exact concentration of active ingredient in a medication sample (gold standard for testing homebrew and counterfeits).

**HRT** — Hormone Replacement (or Hormone Therapy) / Gender-Affirming Hormone Therapy (see **GAHT**).

**Hyperkalemia** — Abnormally high potassium levels in the blood. A potential side effect of spironolactone, especially when combined with NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, or kidney disease.

## I

**IM** — Intramuscular. An injection delivered deep into a muscle (e.g., gluteal, thigh).

**Informed consent** — A model of care where treatment begins after the patient signs a document acknowledging the known risks, without requiring a referral letter from a mental health provider.

**Injection** — Administration of medication via a needle and syringe. Routes include intramuscular (IM) and subcutaneous (SC/SubQ).

## L

**LFT** — Liver function test (see **ALT**, **AST**). Blood tests that measure liver health, typically ALT and AST.

**LH** — Luteinising hormone. Stimulates testosterone production in the testes; suppressed by estrogen therapy.

**Lipid panel** — A blood test measuring cholesterol (HDL, LDL) and triglycerides. Monitored because some HRT routes affect lipid profiles.

## M

**MCT oil** — Medium-chain triglyceride oil. A common carrier oil in homebrew injectables, valued for its low viscosity and stability.

**Meningioma** — A (usually benign) brain tumour associated with high cumulative doses of CPA. Risk increases with dose and duration of use.

**Metoidioplasty** — A surgical procedure that releases the clitoris to create a small phallus; often pursued by trans masculine individuals on T.

**Monotherapy** — Using estrogen alone (without an anti-androgen) at sufficient doses to suppress testosterone via HPG axis feedback.

## N

**NSAIDs** — Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (ibuprofen, naproxen, etc.). Can worsen kidney function and raise potassium when combined with spironolactone.

## P

**P4 / Progesterone** — A hormone involved in menstrual cycles and pregnancy. Used by some trans women for breast maturation, libido, and mood; also used by trans masculine individuals for bleeding control.

**PE** — Pulmonary embolism. A blood clot in the lungs, often originating from a DVT. Life-threatening; requires emergency treatment.

**Pellets** — Subcutaneous hormone implants (typically estradiol or testosterone) that release hormone steadily over several months.

**Polycythemia** — An elevated red blood cell count. In the context of T therapy, this specifically refers to **secondary erythrocytosis** (rising haematocrit from testosterone-stimulated erythropoiesis), which is distinct from polycythemia vera (a bone marrow disorder).

**Prolactin** — A hormone that can become elevated with CPA use. High prolactin may cause galactorrhea (lactation), low libido, and headaches.

**Puberty blocker** — See **GnRH agonist**.

## R

**Relugolix** — A GnRH antagonist used as an anti-androgen. Unlike GnRH agonists, it does not cause an initial testosterone flare. Taken orally once daily.

## S

**SC / SubQ** — Subcutaneous. An injection delivered into the layer of fat under the skin, typically using a shorter, thinner needle than IM.

**SHBG** — Sex hormone-binding globulin. A protein that binds to sex hormones, regulating the amount of free (biologically active) hormone. High SHBG can reduce the effectiveness of HRT, especially with oral estrogen.

**SOC / Standards of Care** — Clinical guidelines published by WPATH (currently Version 8, 2022) for the health of transgender and gender-diverse people.

**Spiro / Spironolactone** — A potassium-sparing diuretic used as an anti-androgen, primarily in the US. Blocks androgen receptors and mildly suppresses T production.

**Sterile technique** — A set of practices (cleaning vial septa with alcohol, using sterile needles, not reusing syringes) used to prevent contamination during injections.

**Sublingual** — A route of administration where a tablet is held under the tongue to dissolve, allowing direct absorption into the bloodstream. Avoids first-pass liver metabolism.

## T

**T** — Testosterone. The primary androgen; used in masculinising HRT; suppressed in feminising HRT.

**Tanner stage** — A scale (1–5) describing progression through puberty. Used to determine appropriate starting doses for adolescent HRT.

**Transdermal** — Absorption through the skin (e.g., patches, gel). Avoids first-pass metabolism; lower VTE risk than oral routes.

**Transfeminine / Trans femme** — A person assigned male at birth who identifies with a feminine gender identity. Used as an umbrella term.

**Transmasculine / Trans masc** — A person assigned female at birth who identifies with a masculine gender identity. Used as an umbrella term.

**Trough** — The lowest point of hormone levels in a dosing cycle (measured just before the next dose). The most clinically useful time to test blood levels.

## U

**Undecylate** — An ester (e.g., estradiol undecylate, EUn) with a long half-life of 12–16 days, allowing dosing every 2–4 weeks.

## V

**Valerate** — An ester (e.g., estradiol valerate, EV) that slows hormone release for multi-day dosing intervals (typically every 5 days for EV).

**Vial** — A small glass bottle with a rubber septum that contains injectable medication. Vials are sealed with a metal crimp cap.

**VTE** — Venous thromboembolism. A collective term for DVT and PE; the most well-known risk of estrogen therapy, especially oral.

## W

**WPATH** — World Professional Association for Transgender Health. An international organisation that publishes the Standards of Care (SOC) for transgender health, currently in Version 8 (2022).
