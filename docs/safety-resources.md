# Safety Resources

Comprehensive harm reduction resources to support safer practices in DIY hormone replacement therapy.

## Essential Safety Guides

### Blood Testing and Monitoring
Regular blood testing is the cornerstone of safe hormone therapy practice. Key tests to monitor include:

**For Transfeminine Individuals:**
- Estradiol (E2) - Target: 100-200 pg/mL (trough)
- Total Testosterone (T) - Target: < 50 ng/dL
- Liver Function Tests (ALT/AST) - Especially important with spironolactone or bicalutamide
- Potassium - Critical when taking spironolactone
- Prolactin - Monitor for elevated levels
- Hemoglobin/Hematocrit - Watch for polycythemia risk
- Lipid Panel - Cholesterol and triglycerides
- SHBG - Sex Hormone Binding Globulin levels

**For Transmasculine Individuals:**
- Total Testosterone (T) - Target: 300-1000 ng/dL (trough)
- Estradiol (E2) - Target: < 50 pg/mL
- Hematocrit/Hemoglobin - Monitor for polycythemia (Hct > 54% concerning)
- Liver Function Tests (ALT/AST)
- Lipid Panel
- Red Blood Cell Count
- Vitamin D levels

### Injection Safety
Proper injection technique reduces risks of infection, pain, and complications:

**Supplies Needed:**
- Appropriate syringe size (1mL for most hormones, 3mL for testosterone undecanoate)
- Correct needle gauge and length (see injection guides)
- Alcohol swabs for skin preparation
- Sharps container for safe disposal
- Bandages or adhesive strips

**Injection Sites:**
- **Intramuscular (IM)**: Thigh (vastus lateralis), hip (ventrogluteal), shoulder (deltoid)
- **Subcutaneous (SubQ)**: Abdomen (away from navel), thigh, upper arm
- **Site rotation**: Essential to prevent lipohypertrophy and scarring

**Technique Tips:**
- Always use aseptic technique
- Never reuse needles or syringes
- Proper disposal in puncture-proof sharps container
- Apply pressure after injection to minimize bruising
- Monitor for signs of infection (redness, swelling, warmth, pus)

### Medication Sourcing and Verification
Reducing risks associated with medication quality and authenticity:

**Supplier Evaluation:**
- Look for community-vetted sources with transparent testing
- Check for batch numbers and expiration dates
- Verify packaging matches legitimate pharmaceutical products
- Start with small test orders when trying new suppliers

**Red Flags to Watch For:**
- Prices that seem too good to be true
- Vendors who refuse to provide product photos or batch information
- Pressure to use untraceable payment methods only
- Numerous reports of under-dosed or contaminated products
- Lack of community reputation or history

**Verification Methods:**
- Compare pills to known legitimate samples (size, color, markings)
- Use reagent test kits when available (for certain compounds)
- Community testing initiatives for hormone concentration
- Visual inspection of packaging quality and seals

### Harm Reduction Practices
Core principles for safer DIY-HRT:

**Start Low, Go Slow**
- Begin with lower doses than standard regimens
- Increase gradually based on blood tests and tolerance
- Allow time for your body to adjust between changes

**One Change at a Time**
- Modify only one medication or dosage at a time
- Wait 4-8 weeks between changes to assess effects
- This helps identify which change caused any observed effects

**Regular Monitoring**
- Schedule blood tests every 3 months initially
- Track symptoms alongside lab results
- Adjust based on both objective data and subjective experience

**Community Engagement**
- Participate in harm reduction forums and groups
- Share experiences (both positive and negative) to help others
- Learn from the experiences of more experienced community members
- Contribute to collective knowledge through reviews and feedback

### Emergency Preparedness
Knowing when to seek immediate medical attention:

**Seek Emergency Care For:**
- Signs of allergic reaction (difficulty breathing, swelling of face/lips/throat, hives)
- Signs of blood clot (sudden leg swelling, pain, redness; chest pain; shortness of breath)
- Signs of infection at injection site (increasing redness, warmth, swelling, pus)
- Severe abdominal pain (especially with spironolactone use)
- Yellowing of skin or eyes (jaundice - possible liver issue)
- Severe depression or suicidal thoughts
- Chest pain or palpitations
- Severe headache with vision changes or neurological symptoms

**When to Contact a Healthcare Provider (Non-Emergency):**
- Persistent side effects that impact quality of life
- Concerns about dosage effectiveness
- Questions about medication interactions
- Need for documentation or referrals
- Routine check-ups and preventive care

## Community Resources

### Testing Collectives
Many communities organize group blood testing to reduce costs:
- Pool resources to purchase multiple tests at discounted rates
- Share transportation costs to testing facilities
- Provide mutual support during the testing process
- Often organized through Discord servers or local meetups

### Educational Materials
- Video tutorials for safe injection techniques
- Guides on proper medication storage and handling
- Calculators for accurate dosing based on concentration
- Interactive tools for tracking symptoms and lab results

### Peer Support Networks
- Online communities for sharing experiences and advice
- Local meetups for in-person support and resource sharing
- Mentorship programs connecting experienced and newer practitioners
- Crisis support lines and mental health resources

## Disclaimer

The information provided here is for educational purposes only and does not constitute medical advice. Always consult with qualified healthcare professionals when possible. The DIY-HRT community encourages harm reduction practices but recognizes that medical supervision is ideal when accessible.

If you experience severe side effects or have concerns about your health, seek immediate medical attention. In case of emergency, call your local emergency services number (e.g., 911 in the United States).
