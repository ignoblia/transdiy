# Progestins

## Overview

Most trans men experience menstrual cessation within 2–6 months of starting testosterone. For those who have persistent bleeding, progestins can help control it.

## When Are Progestins Needed?

- **Breakthrough bleeding** after 6+ months on adequate T therapy
- **Persistent menstruation** despite T levels in target range
- **Bridge therapy** while waiting for T to suppress cycles

::: tip Rule out other causes
Persistent bleeding on T may indicate fibroids, polyps, or other gynaecological issues. If bleeding continues beyond 6 months of adequate T therapy, see a provider for evaluation.
:::

## Available Options

| Medication | Dose | Notes |
|------------|------|-------|
| **Norethindrone** (Aygestin, Primolut-N) | 5 mg/day | Short-term option |
| **Medroxyprogesterone acetate** (Provera) | 5–10 mg/day | Alternative |
| **Depo-Provera** (injectable) | 150 mg every 3 months | Injection |
| **Levonorgestrel IUD** (Mirena) | Long-term | IUD placement |

## Side Effects

| Side Effect | Notes |
|-------------|-------|
| Mood changes | Common with progestins |
| Weight gain | Possible |
| Headaches | Occasional |
| Fatigue | Some report |
