# Starting Doses (Beginner)

## Before You Start

Get a baseline blood test including:
- Total T, free T
- E2
- CBC (especially hematocrit)
- Comprehensive metabolic panel
- Lipid panel

::: info Doses below are for **adults (18+)**.
Adolescent-specific guidance is provided in the section above.
:::

## Adolescent Dosing (Under 18)

Adolescent testosterone therapy follows a **start low, go slow** approach to mimic the gradual pace of endogenous puberty. These guidelines are based on WPATH SOC8 and Endocrine Society (2017) recommendations.

::: warning Important
This guide does not recommend DIY HRT for minors without medical supervision. Hormone therapy in adolescents ideally involves a multidisciplinary team including a qualified provider. The information below is provided for educational purposes and to help adolescents and families understand standard protocols.<a id="ref-adol-1"></a>
:::

### General Principles for Adolescents

- **Pre-pubertal children**: Hormone therapy is **not recommended** — social transition and supportive care are the standard of care <a id="ref-adol-2"></a>.
- **Tanner stage 2+**: Puberty blockers (GnRH agonists) are the first-line intervention if suppression of natal puberty is desired <a id="ref-adol-3"></a>.
- **Gender-affirming hormones** are typically added around age 14–16, after a multidisciplinary team confirms persistence of gender incongruence and the adolescent has sufficient capacity for informed consent <a id="ref-adol-4"></a>.
- The goal is to **mimic pubertal hormone levels**, gradually increasing over 1–2 years until adult doses are reached <a id="ref-adol-5"></a>.

### Testosterone — Pubertal Induction (Early to Mid Puberty)

For younger adolescents (<15 years) or those in early puberty (Tanner stage 2–3), testosterone is introduced at low doses and increased every 6 months. The protocol uses body surface area (mg/m²) to account for growth:

| Phase | Injectable T (Cypionate/Enanthate) | Duration |
|-------|-----------------------------------|----------|
| Starting dose | 25 mg/m² every 2 weeks (or 12.5 mg/m² weekly) | 6 months |
| Step 2 | 50 mg/m² every 2 weeks (or 25 mg/m² weekly) | 6 months |
| Step 3 | 75 mg/m² every 2 weeks (or 37.5 mg/m² weekly) | 6 months |
| Step 4 | 100 mg/m² every 2 weeks (or 50 mg/m² weekly) | 6 months |
| Adult maintenance | 100–200 mg every 2 weeks (or 50–100 mg weekly) | Ongoing |

*For a typical 14-year-old (~1.6 m²), this translates to roughly: start at 20 mg weekly, increase to 40 mg, then 60 mg, then 80 mg weekly over 2 years.*<a id="ref-adol-8"></a>

### Testosterone — Post-Pubertal Adolescents (Tanner Stage 5)

Adolescents who have completed natal puberty can increase doses more rapidly (every 3 months):

| Timeline | Injectable T (Cypionate/Enanthate) | T Gel |
|----------|-----------------------------------|-------|
| Months 0–6 | 75 mg every 2 weeks (37.5 mg/week) | 12.5–25 mg/day |
| Month 6–12 | 125 mg every 2 weeks (62.5 mg/week) | 25–50 mg/day |
| Adult maintenance | 100–200 mg every 2 weeks (50–100 mg/week) | 25–100 mg/day |

### Age-Based Summary

| Age Range | Typical Approach |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Under 13** | Social transition + supportive care. Puberty blockers at Tanner stage 2, if indicated. |
| **13–15** | Puberty blockers if not already started. Low-dose T (pubertal induction protocol). Increase slowly every 6 months. |
| **16–17** | May follow post-pubertal protocol (more rapid increase every 3 months). Full adult doses may be appropriate by age 17–18, guided by levels and response. |
| **18+** | Adult dosing as below. |

## Starting Regimens (Adults)

### Injectable Testosterone

| Ester | Starting Dose | Frequency | Route |
|-------|---------------|-----------|-------|
| T cypionate | 50 mg | Weekly | IM (or SC — see note below) |
| T enanthate | 50 mg | Weekly | IM (or SC — see note below) |

::: tip Subcutaneous (SC) vs Intramuscular (IM)
Both T cypionate and enanthate can be given subcutaneously using a smaller needle (25–27G, 0.5–0.625 inch). Many people find SC less painful and easier to self-administer. Absorption is slightly slower but trough levels remain in target range for most. If switching from IM to SC, the same dose and frequency is a reasonable starting point; confirm with a trough blood test after 4–6 weeks.
:::

### Testosterone Gel

| Brand | Starting Dose | Notes |
|-------|---------------|-------|
| AndroGel 1% | 25–50 mg/day | Apply to shoulders/upper arms |
| Testogel | 25–50 mg/day | Same application area |

### Testosterone Patches

| Brand | Dose | Frequency | Notes |
|-------|------|-----------|-------|
| Androderm | 2–4 mg/24h | Apply daily | Rotate sites (upper arm, thigh, back, abdomen) to avoid skin irritation. Some people develop contact dermatitis; topical corticosteroid cream can help. |

### Testosterone Pellets (Testopel)

| Dose | Frequency | Notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| 600–900 mg (6–9 pellets, 75 mg each) | Every 3–6 months | Inserted subcutaneously in a minor office procedure. Provides steady levels with no daily/weekly dosing. Requires a healthcare provider for insertion and removal. |

### Buccal Tablets (Striant)

| Dose | Frequency | Notes |
|------|-----------|-------|
| 30 mg | Twice daily (every 12 hours) | Applied to the upper gum above the incisor. Tablet dissolves over several hours. Can cause gum irritation. |

## Sample Regimens

```
T cypionate 50 mg IM weekly
```
or
```
T gel 50 mg daily
```
or
```
T patch 4 mg/24h daily (rotate sites)
```

## When to Increase

- **After 4–8 weeks:** Check T levels at trough
- **Target:** 300–1,000 ng/dL
- If below target and tolerating well, increase dose by 25–50%
