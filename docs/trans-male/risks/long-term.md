# Long-Term Risks

## Polycythemia (High Red Blood Cell Count)

The most common significant side effect of testosterone therapy.

| HCT Level | Risk | Action |
|-----------|------|--------|
| < 50% | Normal | Routine monitoring |
| 50–54% | Moderate | Consider blood donation |
| > 54% | **High** | Reduce T dose or therapeutic phlebotomy |

## Cardiovascular Effects

| Effect | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| Blood pressure | T can raise BP in some individuals |
| HDL cholesterol | T typically lowers HDL |
| LDL cholesterol | Variable effect |
| Heart attack risk | Not clearly elevated in trans men |
| Stroke risk | Low absolute risk |

## Sleep Apnoea

Testosterone can worsen pre-existing sleep apnoea. If you have sleep apnoea, monitor symptoms.

## Cancer Risk

| Cancer Type | Risk on T |
|-------------|-----------|
| Breast cancer | Risk decreases (T suppresses E2) |
| Ovarian cancer | No clear increased risk |
| Uterine/endometrial | Uncertain — limited data |
| Prostate | N/A (no prostate in AFAB individuals) |

## Bone Health

Testosterone is protective of bone density. Trans men on T have normal to above-normal bone density.
