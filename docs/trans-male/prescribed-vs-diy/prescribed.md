# Prescribed HRT (Trans Male)

## What Is Prescribed HRT?

Prescribed HRT means obtaining testosterone through a licensed healthcare provider — typically an endocrinologist, primary care physician, or specialised gender clinic, with regular blood tests and dose adjustments.

## Routes to Access

### Informed Consent Model

Under informed consent, treatment begins without requiring a therapist's letter or formal diagnosis.

- **Common in:** United States (Planned Parenthood, LGBTQ+ clinics), parts of Europe
- **Pros:** Fast access; minimal gatekeeping
- **Cons:** Not available everywhere

### Traditional Model

You obtain a diagnosis of gender dysphoria from a mental health professional.

- **Common in:** UK (NHS GICs), many European countries, parts of Asia
- **Pros:** Structured care
- **Cons:** Long waiting lists (up to 5–7 years)

### Private Healthcare

- **Pros:** Shorter waiting times, more choice
- **Cons:** Expensive

## Common Prescribed Regimens

| Form | Typical Prescription |
|------|---------------------|
| T cypionate (injectable) | 50–100 mg/week or 100–200 mg/2 weeks |
| T enanthate (injectable) | 50–100 mg/week or 100–200 mg/2 weeks |
| T gel | 25–100 mg/day |
| T patches | 2–6 mg/day |
| T pellets | 750–1,500 mg every 3–6 months |

## Pros of Prescribed HRT

- Medical supervision and regular blood tests
- Pharmaceutical-grade quality-controlled medication
- Legal protection (T is a controlled substance in most countries)
- May be covered by insurance
- Monitoring for haematocrit, lipids, liver function

## Cons of Prescribed HRT

- Long waiting lists (especially UK NHS)
- Gatekeeping by some providers
- Cost without insurance
- Some providers use inflexible dosing protocols
