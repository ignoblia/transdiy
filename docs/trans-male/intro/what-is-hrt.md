# What is HRT? (Trans Male Guide)

**HRT** stands for **Hormone Replacement Therapy**. For trans males, it is a medical treatment that helps align your physical characteristics with your gender identity through the use of **testosterone**.

## How HRT Works

Masculinising hormone therapy (GAHT) for trans men typically involves:

1. **Introducing testosterone** — Taking testosterone to raise levels into the cisgender male range
2. **Suppressing estrogen** — Testosterone naturally suppresses estrogen production through HPG axis feedback
3. **Monitoring for side effects** — Regular blood tests to manage hematocrit, lipids, and other markers

Testosterone therapy induces male-puberty-like changes: voice deepening, facial and body hair growth, fat redistribution, muscle mass increase, and cessation of menstruation.

## What HRT Does (and Doesn't) Do

### Physical Changes

::: warning Timelines are highly individual
The ranges below are population-level observations, not predictions for any one person. Onset and progression depend heavily on your regimen, dosage, age, genetics, and overall health.
:::

| Change | Typical Onset | Typical Peak | Reversible? |
|--------|---------------|--------------|-------------|
| Voice deepening | 3–6 months | 1–2 years | **No** |
| Facial/body hair growth | 3–6 months | 3–5 years | **No** (partially) |
| Menstrual cessation | 2–6 months | 6–12 months | **Yes** (returns if T stops) |
| Clitoral growth (bottom growth) | 1–3 months | 1–2 years | **No** |
| Fat redistribution | 3–6 months | 2–5 years | **Yes** |
| Muscle mass increase | 3–6 months | 2–3 years | **Yes** |
| Skin oiliness/acne | 1–3 months | 1–2 years | **Yes** |
| Body odour change | 1–3 months | 3–6 months | **Yes** |
| Scalp hair loss (male pattern) | 6–12 months | 2–5 years | **Partially** |
| Increased libido | 1–3 months | 3–6 months | **Yes** |

::: danger Fertility Warning
Masculinising HRT will likely reduce your fertility, but the effect varies. HRT is **not** a contraceptive — use a reliable method of birth control if relevant to you.
:::

::: info Gamete Storage
If you are considering having biological children in the future, discuss egg/ovarian tissue storage with a healthcare provider **before** starting HRT.
:::

### What HRT Does NOT Do

- **Change your bone structure** — height, hand/foot size, and hip width are unchanged
- **Remove breast tissue** — testosterone does not significantly reduce breast size; top surgery is required
- **Guarantee fertility preservation** — HRT reduces fertility; permanent infertility is possible
- **Guarantee voice drop** — the extent of voice deepening varies

## Key Hormone Targets & Monitoring

For masculinising GAHT, the Endocrine Society and WPATH SOC8 recommend targeting:

- **Testosterone (T)** — in the cisgender male range, typically **300–1,000 ng/dL**
- **Estradiol (E2)** — should be suppressed, ideally below 50 pg/mL
- **Hematocrit (HCT)** — must stay below 54% (risk of polycythemia)

The Endocrine Society recommends monitoring hormone levels **every 3 months** during the first year of therapy.

## Common Medications for Masculinising HRT

### Testosterone Options

| Form | Examples | Typical Dose | Notes |
|------|----------|-------------|-------|
| Injections (IM/SC) | Testosterone cypionate, enanthate | 50–100 mg/week or 100–200 mg/every 2 weeks | Most common; steady levels with weekly dosing |
| Gel/cream | AndroGel, Testogel | 25–100 mg/day | Good for stable levels; transfer risk to others |
| Patches | Androderm | 2–6 mg/day | Can cause skin irritation |
| Pellets (implants) | Testopel | 750–1,500 mg every 3–6 months | Long-lasting; requires insertion procedure |
| Buccal tablets | Striant | 30 mg twice daily | Adheres to gum; least common |

### Estrogen Blockers (Aromatase Inhibitors)

| Medication | Typical Dose | Notes |
|------------|-------------|-------|
| Anastrozole | 1 mg/week | Used if E2 is elevated despite adequate T |
| Letrozole | 2.5 mg/week | Alternative to anastrozole |

### Progestins (for Bleeding Control)

| Medication | Typical Dose | Notes |
|------------|-------------|-------|
| Norethindrone | 5 mg/day | Short-term option for persistent bleeding |
| Medroxyprogesterone acetate | 10 mg/day | Alternative option |

## Side Effects & Risks

### Polycythemia (High Red Blood Cell Count)

The most common significant side effect of testosterone therapy. Testosterone stimulates erythropoietin, increasing red blood cell production.

| HCT Level | Risk | Action |
|-----------|------|--------|
| < 50% | Normal | Routine monitoring |
| 50–54% | Moderate | Monitor; consider blood donation |
| > 54% | **High** | Reduce T dose or therapeutic phlebotomy |

### Other Side Effects

- **Acne** — very common, especially in first 1–2 years
- **Male pattern balding** — genetic predisposition determines extent
- **Sleep apnoea** — testosterone may worsen pre-existing sleep apnoea
- **Lipid changes** — T can lower HDL and raise LDL
- **Increased libido** — expected and usually welcome
- **Mood changes** — irritability or aggression (dose-dependent)
- **Weight gain** — mostly lean muscle mass

## DIY vs Prescribed HRT

### Prescribed HRT
- Under supervision of an endocrinologist or knowledgeable provider
- Regular blood tests and dose adjustments
- **Pros:** safer, medically supervised, legal protection
- **Cons:** long waiting lists, gatekeeping, cost

### DIY HRT
- Obtaining testosterone without a prescription
- **Pros:** immediate access, full control
- **Cons:** requires self-education, blood testing, reputable sourcing; testosterone is a controlled substance in most countries

::: warning If you use DIY HRT, you must:
1. **Test your blood regularly** — every 3 months during the first year
2. **Source from reputable vendors** — see the Sources section
3. **Educate yourself thoroughly** — understand dosing, side effects, and emergency signs
4. **Be aware of legal risks** — testosterone is a controlled substance in many jurisdictions
:::

## Is HRT Reversible?

**Some effects are permanent:**
- Voice deepening — permanent
- Bottom growth — permanent
- Facial/body hair — permanent but may thin if T stops
- Male pattern baldness — permanent (hair loss reversal unlikely)

**Most other changes reverse if HRT is stopped:**
- Fat redistribution, muscle mass, skin changes, menstruation return, and fertility generally reverse over time
