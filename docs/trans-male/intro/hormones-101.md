# Hormones 101: Testosterone

## What Are Hormones?

Hormones are chemical messengers in your body. They travel through your bloodstream and tell cells what to do — like "grow facial hair" or "deepen the voice".

For trans masculine HRT, we focus on three main players:

1. **Testosterone** — the primary masculinising hormone
2. **Estrogen** — the primary feminising hormone we want to suppress
3. **Progestins** — used optionally for bleeding control

## Testosterone

Testosterone is the primary male sex hormone. It belongs to a class of hormones called **androgens**.

### What Testosterone Does

| Effect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Voice deepening | Thickens vocal cords, enlarges larynx |
| Facial/body hair growth | Converts vellus hair to terminal hair |
| Muscle mass increase | Promotes protein synthesis |
| Fat redistribution | Shifts fat from hips/thighs to abdomen |
| Bottom growth | Enlarges clitoral tissue |
| Menstrual cessation | Suppresses ovarian cycle |
| Bone density | Maintains bone mass |
| Libido | Increases sexual desire |
| Red blood cells | Stimulates erythropoiesis |

### The Testosterone Molecule

Testosterone's chemical formula is **C₁₉H₂₈O₂** (molar mass: 288.42 g/mol). It is a steroid hormone built on a four-ring carbon backbone.

Key features:
- **Ketone (=O) at C3** on ring A
- **Hydroxyl (–OH) at C17β** on ring D
- **Methyl groups (–CH₃) at C18 and C19**

### How Testosterone Works

Testosterone enters cells and binds to **androgen receptors** (AR), activating genes that drive masculine development. It can also be converted to:

- **DHT (dihydrotestosterone)** — a much more potent androgen, responsible for facial hair, body hair, and male pattern baldness
- **Estradiol** — via the aromatase enzyme; a small amount of T is always converted to E2

## DHT: The Potent Metabolite

DHT is approximately 2–3× more potent than testosterone (some sources cite up to 5× depending on tissue and assay). It is produced by the enzyme **5α-reductase** converting T to DHT in specific tissues (skin, scalp, prostate).

| Effect | Mostly T | Mostly DHT |
|--------|----------|------------|
| Voice deepening | ✅ | |
| Muscle mass | ✅ | |
| Bottom growth | ✅ | ✅ |
| Facial hair | | ✅ |
| Body hair | | ✅ |
| Scalp hair loss | | ✅ |
| Libido | ✅ | ✅ |

## The HPG Axis in Trans Men

The **hypothalamus–pituitary–gonadal (HPG) axis** controls hormone production:

1. **Hypothalamus** releases GnRH
2. **Pituitary** responds with LH and FSH
3. **Ovaries** produce estrogen in response to FSH

Exogenous testosterone suppresses this axis via negative feedback:

- High T → hypothalamus reduces GnRH → pituitary reduces LH/FSH → ovaries produce less estrogen
- This is why menstruation typically stops within 2–6 months of starting T

## Estrogen

In trans men, high estrogen is undesirable because it opposes masculinisation. Testosterone therapy naturally suppresses E2 in most people.

| E2 Level | Interpretation |
|----------|---------------|
| < 50 pg/mL | Well-suppressed (typical on T therapy) |
| 50–100 pg/mL | Borderline |
| > 100 pg/mL | Elevated; may need intervention |

If E2 remains high despite adequate T levels, an aromatase inhibitor (anastrozole, letrozole) may be considered.

## Sex Hormone Binding Globulin (SHBG)

SHBG binds to sex hormones and regulates how much is "free" (active). Testosterone lowers SHBG, which means:

- More free T is available at the same total T level
- Trans men on T typically have lower SHBG than cis men at the same total T

## Hormone Level Targets

| Hormone | Target Range | Purpose |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| Total T | 300–1,000 ng/dL | Masculinisation |
| Free T | 50–200 pg/mL | Activity marker |
| E2 | < 50 pg/mL | Suppress feminising effects |
| SHBG | 10–50 nmol/L | Binding protein |
| Hematocrit | < 54% | Prevent polycythemia |

## Summary

| Hormone | Role in Trans Masculine HRT | How We Modify It |
|---------|----------------------------|-----------------|
| **Testosterone** | Primary masculinising hormone | Added via injections, gel, patches, pellets, buccal |
| **Estrogen** | Feminising hormone to suppress | Suppressed by T therapy |
| **DHT** | Potent androgen (hair, bottom growth) | Produced from T; finasteride blocks conversion |
| **SHBG** | Binds sex hormones; regulates free T | Decreases on T therapy |
| **LH/FSH** | Signals ovaries to produce E2 | Suppressed by exogenous T |
